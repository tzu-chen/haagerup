01The symmetry

A symmetry with an irrational size

The Haagerup category has six members. Three of them act like the rotations of a triangle. The other three each have size (3 + √13)/2.

Ordinary symmetries form groups. Do two of them and you get a third, and every one can be undone. The Haagerup fusion category, which grew out of a subfactor that Uffe Haagerup identified in the early 1990s, has six simple objects that combine differently. Three of them, 1, α and α2, compose like the rotations of a triangle, with α3 = 1. The other three obey ρ ⊗ ρ = 1 ⊕ ρ ⊕ αρ ⊕ α2ρ. Combining ρ with itself produces four objects at once, and nothing undoes it.

Each object has a size, its quantum dimension. Sizes multiply under ⊗ and add under ⊕, so the rule for ρ forces d2 = 1 + 3d, and d = (3 + √13)/2 ≈ 3.303. A group element always has size one. That is why this symmetry does not fit inside any group, and why it has been hard to find in quantum field theory.

In the render each object is a sphere. A line joins two objects when one of them appears in ρ times the other. The sizes are not chosen by hand: they are the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of this graph, and its eigenvalue is d. The ring around each teal sphere is a self-loop. It records that ρ occurs inside ρ ⊗ ρ.

In the manuscript these objects become topological lines in a unitary conformal field theory at c = 8. The chosen E8 extension of the chiral algebra carries them, and a line can be wrapped around space and read as an operator. The line ρ multiplies the vacuum sector by d and the next sector, b, by −1/d.

02The hiding place

Hiding inside E8

The theory is built inside the most symmetric lattice in eight dimensions. Its 248 currents split three ways, and whether two of the parts agree decided the shape of the whole construction.

The E8 lattice gives a conformal field theory at central charge 8 with 248 conserved currents: eight along the Cartan directions and one for each of the 240 roots drawn here. The picture is the classic projection in which the roots fall on eight rings of thirty.

The construction starts one step down, in the sublattice E6 ⊕ A2 of index three. Its 78 roots are gold. The rest of E8 splits into two cosets of 81 roots each, teal and coral. From the gold part the manuscript picks six currents, an sl2 at level 1 and an sl2 at level 39, whose roots are ringed in white. With the stress tensor and one more field u they generate the chiral algebra U.

Seen from U, the 248 currents regroup as 6 + 80 + 81 + 81. The 6 belong to U. The 80 fill out the rest of the gold part. The teal and coral 81s start two sectors, Y and Y*.

The proof then turns on a yes-or-no question. Are Y and Y* the same? If they differ, the symmetry left over has four objects. If they agree, it has six. The manuscript rules out four, so teal and coral carry the same sector. Further arguments identify the six-object ring as Haagerup, and the chosen E8 extension restricts to U as M0 ⊕ Mb ⊕ 2Ma.

03The seed

One field, three waves

Six currents and one extra field generate the whole theory. The field is a sum of three waves, and the arithmetic fact 13 + 27 = 40 fixes the phase between them.

A lattice theory is built from waves. Each lattice vector a gives a field ea that behaves like a plane wave with momentum a. The defining field of the construction adds three of them: u = ea1 + ((√13 + 3√−3)/20) ea2 + ((√13 − 3√−3)/20) ea3.

Each momentum has squared length 4, and each pair meets at 60°. Together with the origin they form a regular tetrahedron. Measured from their centre, they are three wave vectors 120° apart. Three such waves interfere in a honeycomb. At the dark points the waves cancel, and the phase, shown as colour, winds once around each of them.

In the theory's positive inner product the three coefficients become 1, ζ and ζ, with ζ = (√13 + 3i√3)/√40. Its length is exactly 1 because 13 + 27 = 40. So there is no dial left to turn. The field carries both signature numbers of the theory, √13 from the Haagerup dimension and √−3 from the triangle of invertible objects. Its charges under the two current algebras are 1 and 13.

Everything else is computed inside the lattice theory. U has 6 states of weight one, 120 of weight two and 956 of weight three. The hard part of the proof is showing that U is proper: that no singular product of its higher fields ever creates a new current, at any weight. A finite strong-generation criterion settles this using exact identities over ℚ(√13, √−3).

04The core

Five squared is minus one

Deep inside the algebra sits a core glued from two well-known theories. The glue exists because 5 × 5 = 25 is one less than 2 × 13.

To control every module of U at once, the proof looks for a smaller algebra inside it that is already understood. It finds one made of two pieces: a lattice–Potts core of central charge 14/5, and the level-13 parafermions of central charge 8/5. Each piece has simple currents labelled by residues mod 13. Together their labels (j, k) fill the 13 × 13 grid on this torus.

Two such currents can be glued into a larger algebra only if the combined spin is a whole number. For the label (j, k) the spin is −(j2 + k2)/13 up to integers, so gluing needs j2 + k2 divisible by 13. Because 52 = 25 ≡ −1 (mod 13), the solutions are exactly the lines k = −5j and k = 5j. On the torus each line becomes a knot that goes once around the long way and five times around the tube.

The two lines cannot both be used, because together they generate labels with fractional spin. An exact field certificate supplies weight-two vectors in U with charge (5, 1), which lies on the coral line k = −5j. With the manuscript's orientation convention, that is the line realized. The result is a subalgebra whose representation category is Fib ⊠ SO(3)13, with fourteen simple modules.

This core is what bounds the lowest weight of every U-module by 17/10, without assuming unitarity. That bound is the first step in pinning down all twelve sectors. The final lowest weights top out at 15/13.

05The fingerprint

Thirteen hours

The theory has twelve kinds of particle. On a clock with thirteen hours, six of them sit exactly on the hours that are not perfect squares.

A rational conformal field theory sorts its states into finitely many sectors. Here there are twelve, the simple objects of the Drinfeld center Z(H3). Each has a twist, a phase set by its conformal weight. On a 13-hour clock the six sectors d1, …, d6 land at hours 6, 11, 2, 5, 7 and 8. These are exactly the non-squares mod 13. The squares 1, 3, 4, 9, 10 and 12 are the empty sockets. Two more sectors sit on a three-hour dial inside, and four with trivial twist sit at the centre.

Squares and non-squares mod 13 are the ingredients of a Gauss sum. Take twelve unit steps, the j-th in the direction of hour j: forwards if j is a square, backwards if not. The coral steps are the backward ones, at the same hours as the coral beads. The walk ends exactly at √13. The manuscript's defect code builds √13 this way. It then checks the modular data in exact arithmetic over ℚ(ζ13): S2 = 1, (SΘ)3 = 1, and all 1,728 fusion coefficients are non-negative integers.

The clock also fixes the characters. The modular data allow a family of character vectors, built from Evans and Gannon's fundamental matrix and indexed by an integer γ ≥ 0, and the member γ has 6 + 13γ currents. The construction has exactly six, so γ = 0, and the whole vector follows. The vacuum character begins q−1/3(1 + 6q + 120q2 + 956q3 + 6010q4 + ⋯).

One subtlety: the twist of d3 points to hour 2, but its lowest weight is 15/13, not 2/13. The clock shows only the phase; the weight goes one full turn further.

06The interaction

The shape of a four-point function

Modular data say which particles exist. Correlators say how they interact. This is the first exact one, drawn as a landscape.

Place the field u and its conjugate at 0, x, 1 and infinity. The four-point function is then an exact rational function, G0(x) = (3 − 4x + 4x2) / (3x4(1 − x)4). Each field is a sum of three exponentials, so the product has 81 terms. Charge conservation kills all but 15, and each survivor carries weight 1/9.

The landscape shows its size as height, on a log scale, and its phase as colour, over the complex x-plane. Where two operators collide, at x = 0 and x = 1, there are poles of order four. They rise as glowing calderas, and the colours wind four times around each. The two sinkholes at x = ½ ± i/√2 are the zeros. The mirror symmetry across Re x = ½ is crossing symmetry, G0(1 − x) = G0(x).

Expanding in conformal blocks gives the channel data 1, 8/3, 14/3, 58/9, 107/15, … The 8/3 = 1/2 + 13/6 is the exchange of the two Cartan currents, with the charges 1 and 13 again. The 14/3 splits as 1 for the stress tensor plus 11/3 for the other weight-two primaries. The same 11/3 comes out independently from the full table of 113 × 113 × 113 three-point couplings.

Data like these go beyond the torus partition function. A lattice model or a bootstrap study would need them to recognise this particular theory.

07The physics

Two theories, one central charge

The same chiral algebra builds two different worlds. Only one of them can be pushed off criticality by a relevant scalar.

A chiral algebra does not by itself fix a full theory. Left and right movers still have to be paired. Pair each of U's twelve sectors with itself and you get the diagonal theory on the left. Extend U to E8 on both sides and you get the E8 theory with Haagerup lines on the right. Both have c = 8 in each chirality. They have the same free energy, −4πT2/3v per unit length, and the same entanglement coefficient, 8/3.

Their spectra are very different. Each bar stands at a pair of conformal weights (h, h̄), and its height counts the Virasoro primaries there, from the exact spectrum computed through dimension 24. The diagonal theory crowds bars at thirds and thirteenths. Below the coral line Δ = 2, seven glowing scalar towers hold 9, 25, 49, 169, 196, 729 and 1,764 relevant scalars, 2,941 in all. The E8 theory has integer weights only, and no scalar below the line.

The difference is physical. Relevant scalars are the knobs that drive a critical system away from criticality. In the ordinary modular bootstrap of Collier, Lin and Yin, the E8 theory sits at the kink at (c, Δ) = (8, 2), while the diagonal theory sits deep inside the allowed region at (8, 2/3).

Yet the symmetry sees what they share. Keep only the states that every symmetry line leaves alone: the twelve Verlinde lines on the left, the Haagerup lines of the first E8 factor on the right. In both, the first such scalar has dimension 2, with 36 components on the left and 1,488 on the right.

How these were made

Views 1, 4, 6 and 7 are Blender Cycles renders. Views 2, 3 and 5 are drawn as additive light in NumPy. All seven share one finishing pass: bloom, ACES tone mapping and film grain. Every mark is placed from exact data: the manuscript's Gram matrix and defining field, its modular data, the exported character and spectrum tables, and the exact four-point function.
