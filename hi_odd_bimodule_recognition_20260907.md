# Odd-order recognition from one intertwiner

Research note, 7 September 2026. This is a conditional recognition theorem,
with a proof, for the infinitude problem in
`haagerup_izumi_class_field_stark_notes.md`. Its hypotheses have **not** been
verified for a uniform family of the phase candidates. It does not establish
infinitude. This note was developed using mathematical reasoning and primary
literature while local computation scripts were paused; no computational
verification is claimed.

## 1. Recognition in a category that may initially have infinite depth

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group of odd order \(n\), and set

\[
d=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^2+4}}2,\qquad d^2=1+nd.
\]

Let \(\mathcal D\) be a semisimple rigid \(C^*\)-tensor category with simple
unit, finite-dimensional morphism spaces, finite direct sums and split
projections. We do not assume that it has finitely many simple objects.
Use its positive intrinsic dimension. Assume:

1. There is a fully faithful unitary tensor embedding of the untwisted
   pointed category \(\operatorname{Vec}_G\), with simple objects
   \(\alpha_g\).
2. There is a self-dual simple object \(\rho\) of dimension \(d\), and
   the \(n\) objects \(\alpha_g\rho\) are mutually inequivalent.
3. There are isomorphisms
   \(\alpha_g\rho\cong\rho\alpha_{-g}\).
4. There is a nonzero actual morphism
   \(v:\rho\longrightarrow\rho^2\) in \(\mathcal D\).

**Proposition.** The full tensor subcategory generated by the \(\alpha_g\)
and \(\rho\) is a unitary Haagerup–Izumi fusion category. In particular,

\[
\rho^2\cong\mathbf1\oplus\bigoplus_{g\in G}\alpha_g\rho.
\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** Conjugation by an invertible object,

\[
F_a(X)=\alpha_a X\alpha_{-a},
\]

is a unitary tensor autoequivalence. Covariance gives

\[
F_a(\rho)\cong\alpha_{2a}\rho,
\qquad F_a(\rho^2)\cong\rho^2.
\]

For the second identity, first observe
\(\alpha_a\rho^2\cong\rho^2\alpha_a\). Applying \(F_a\) to \(v\)
therefore gives a nonzero morphism
\(\alpha_{2a}\rho\to\rho^2\). Doubling is a bijection of \(G\), so all
the \(n\) distinct simple objects \(\alpha_g\rho\) occur in \(\rho^2\).
Self-duality and simplicity of \(\rho\) give one copy of the tensor unit.
These objects are distinct from the invertibles since \(d>1\).

The displayed summands have total dimension \(1+nd=d^2\), exactly the
dimension of \(\rho^2\). Positivity of dimension forces their multiplicities
to be one and excludes every additional summand. This proves (1).
Covariance then gives

\[
(\alpha_g\rho)(\alpha_h\rho)
\cong\alpha_{g-h}\oplus\bigoplus_{k\in G}\alpha_k\rho.
\]

Together with the pointed products, these identities show that the
\(2n\) specified simple objects are closed under tensor products and duals.
Their full subcategory is therefore a fusion category. Its associativity
and pentagon are inherited from \(\mathcal D\), and its pointed subcategory
is untwisted by assumption. \(\square\)

This argument needs actual morphisms and positive dimensions. It cannot be
applied directly to a list of proposed fusion coefficients or to an
uncompleted associator. Oddness is used at the precise step where
\(a\mapsto2a\) must cover the whole group.

## 2. A rank-two Q-system supplies the intertwiner

Retain hypotheses 1–3. Suppose that
\(A=\mathbf1\oplus\rho\) is the underlying object of a normalized standard
Q-system in \(\mathcal D\). Here **Q-system means an associative special
\(C^*\)-Frobenius algebra object**, not the polynomial quadratic identities
called \(Q\) in the main research notes.

Write \(m:A\otimes A\to A\) for multiplication and take the unit
\(u:\mathbf1\to A\) to be an isometry. In this normalization,

\[
mm^*=(1+d)\operatorname{id}_A.
\tag{2}
\]

The normalization is the one in Definition 2.17 and Remark 2.18 of
[Carpi–Gaudio–Giorgetti–Hillier, *Haploid Algebras in
\(C^*\)-Tensor Categories and the Schellekens List*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-023-04722-9).

Let \(i:\rho\to A\) be the inclusion and let
\(b=i^*m(i\otimes i):\rho^2\to\rho\). Restrict (2) to its \(\rho\)
output block. The input decomposition is

\[
A^2=(\mathbf1\otimes\mathbf1)\oplus
(\mathbf1\otimes\rho)\oplus(\rho\otimes\mathbf1)\oplus\rho^2.
\]

The first block contributes zero; the next two each contribute
\(\operatorname{id}_\rho\) by the unit laws. Consequently

\[
bb^*=(d-1)\operatorname{id}_\rho.
\tag{3}
\]

Since \(d>1\),

\[
v=(d-1)^{-1/2}b^*:\rho\longrightarrow\rho^2
\]

is an isometry. Thus hypothesis 4 follows, and the proposition applies.
Conjugating this isometry supplies the inclusions of all the
\(\alpha_g\rho\) in (1); a normalized coevaluation supplies the unit
inclusion. Their orthogonal range projections sum to the identity by
the same dimension argument.

## 3. A sufficient bimodule construction and the full principal graph

Let \(X\) be an irreducible finite-index \(N\)-\(M\) bimodule between
\(\mathrm{II}_1\) factors, of index \(d+1\). Suppose

\[
X\bar X\cong\mathbf1_N\oplus\rho
\]

with \(\rho\) simple. Suppose an honest outer action of \(G\) on \(N\)
gives invertible bimodules \(\alpha_g\), with a free orbit on \(\rho\)
and inversion covariance as in hypotheses 2–3. Then the tensor category
generated by these bimodules is Haagerup–Izumi.

Indeed, \(X\bar X\) has its canonical normalized standard Q-system,
\(\rho\) is self-dual and has dimension \(d\), and the preceding argument
applies. The needed trivalent intertwiner comes from this canonical
algebra, independently of any proposed coefficient formula for it.

The principal graph of \(X\) is also forced. Put
\(X_g=\alpha_gX\) and let \(Y\) be the complement of \(X\) in \(\rho X\).
This complement exists because Frobenius reciprocity gives

\[
\langle X,\rho X\rangle=1.
\]

Here \(\langle U,V\rangle=\dim\operatorname{Hom}(U,V)\). Once (1) is
known, reciprocity gives

\[
\langle\rho X,\rho X\rangle
=\langle\rho^2,\mathbf1\oplus\rho\rangle=2.
\]

Hence \(\rho X=X\oplus Y\) with \(Y\) simple. The \(X_g\) are mutually
inequivalent, since

\[
\langle X_g,X_h\rangle
=\langle\alpha_{h-g},\mathbf1\oplus\rho\rangle=\delta_{g,h}.
\]

Also \(\langle X_g,Y\rangle=0\): both
\(\langle X_g,\rho X\rangle\) and \(\langle X_g,X\rangle\) equal
\(\delta_{g,0}\). In the Grothendieck group,

\[
Y\bar X=\rho^2-\mathbf1=\bigoplus_g\alpha_g\rho.
\]

Reciprocity now gives
\(\langle\alpha_gY,Y\rangle
=\langle\alpha_g\rho,Y\bar X\rangle=1\), so
\(\alpha_gY\cong Y\). Thus the complete adjacency rules are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_gX&=X_g, &
(\alpha_g\rho)X&=X_g\oplus Y,\\
X_g\bar X&=\alpha_g\oplus\alpha_g\rho, &
Y\bar X&=\bigoplus_g\alpha_g\rho.
\end{aligned}
\]

The graph has the \(n\) length-three arms
\(\alpha_g-X_g-\alpha_g\rho-Y\), with distinguished endpoint
\(\alpha_0\). In particular, simplicity of \(Y\) is a conclusion, not
an additional hypothesis of this criterion.

The finite-dimensional fusion-counting method is motivated by
[Asaeda–Haagerup, *Exotic subfactors of finite depth*, Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9803044),
which treats the order-three construction. The argument above states and
proves an odd-order criterion using the canonical Q-system to obtain the
initial nonzero intertwiner.

## 4. What this does and does not resolve

A possible sufficient construction target is therefore an irreducible
bimodule \(X\) of index \(d+1\), the simple complement \(\rho\), an honest
outer cyclic action, a free orbit on \(\rho\), and an actual covariance
isomorphism. No such family has been constructed here for infinitely many
orders. In particular, neither positivity at a physical embedding nor the
required bimodule covariance follows just by calling a phase coboundary
an intertwiner.

Two literature checks explain why existing flatness results do not
currently fill this gap:

- [Kawahigashi's 2026 zipper theorem, Theorem 4.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11005-026-02069-5)
  equates half zipper, zipper, half flatness and flatness for a given field
  of strings. An intertwining condition still has to be proved. The theorem
  does not infer it from biunitarity alone.
- In [Izumi's classification paper, Lemmas 3.14–3.15 and Theorem 4.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/36852),
  the tensor-unit intertwiner and the noninvertible intertwiners have
  different proofs. The latter invoke the cubic equations. Thus the first
  part of that construction does not justify discarding the remaining
  cubic relations.

The existing algebraic route through the phase equations remains open.
This recognition theorem offers a separate, precisely stated sufficient
construction route; it is not an application to the current uniform
candidates and is not a proof of infinitude.
