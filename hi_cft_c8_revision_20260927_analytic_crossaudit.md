## Reconstruction and common domains

The primary input is
[Henriques--Tener, Section 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20735v3#S9).
Remark 9.8 explicitly extends both Proposition 9.3 and Theorem 9.5 to the
bounded-energy admissible module even without discrete energy spectrum.
Definition 9.4 defines the smeared field by closing its bounded-energy
restriction. Thus the domain used in the projection argument is a core
for the operator identified by Theorem 9.5(ii). The argument in Corollary
9.6 identifies the two Virasoro actions by localized smearing and a
partition of unity; it remains valid on this domain. Theorem 8.9 applies
to the strongly local vacuum VOA and identifies its reconstruction with
the original `U`.

Regularity therefore supplies an ordinary simple summand `S` of the
reconstructed admissible module before finite multiplicities of the net
sector have been proved. Since each ordinary energy space of `S` is
finite-dimensional, its Hilbert closure reduces the self-adjoint
Hamiltonian. The projection onto that closure sends each bounded-energy
space into a finite algebraic sum of `S` energy spaces. This proves the
required domain invariance without assuming finite multiplicities for
the entire net sector.

The invariant Hermitian form then gives mode commutation with the
projection, including commutation with the formal adjoint modes. The
vector-valued smeared fields converge on the bounded-energy domain,
so boundedness of the projection transports that commutation to the
smearing. The common-core lemma extends it to closed operators and
their adjoints, hence their polar partial isometries and spectral
projections. Normal local representations preserve the von Neumann
algebras generated by these bounded constituents. Consequently the
projection is a net intertwiner and irreducibility forces `S` to fill
the entire sector. This discharges the possible infinite-multiplicity
obstruction rather than assuming it away.

For fullness, one should justify uniqueness of invariant positive forms
algebraically before claiming boundedness of the module intertwiner.
Given two forms on an ordinary simple module, compare them separately
on each finite-dimensional energy space. The resulting grade-preserving
operator is an algebraic `U`-endomorphism by invariance. Choose an
eigenvector on the finite-dimensional lowest space. Its eigenspace is a
nonzero submodule, hence the whole module. Thus the comparison operator
is one positive scalar in every grade. Only now does an isomorphism of
ordinary unitary modules admit a bounded unitary rescaling. Its extension
intertwines closed fields because the finite-energy restrictions are
common cores. This supports the claimed fully faithful linear functor;
it does not supply a tensor structure.

## The actual inclusion and finite index

[Tener, Corollary 4.24](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-024-00685-8)
has exactly the required specialization: a regular unitary conformal
subalgebra of a simple unitary VOA with bounded localized vertex
operators gives finite index for the bounded-insertion net inclusion.
His [Lemma 7.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08168#S7.SS1.SSS4) transfers
the comparison with the smeared-field net to unitary subalgebras, but
only up to net isomorphism. The revised argument uses only complete
rationality at this step, so this limitation causes no problem.

For the *actual* smeared-field inclusion, a local relative-commutant
element `x in N_H(I) intersect N_U(I)'` also commutes with `N_U(I')`
by locality. Strong additivity expresses crossing intervals using
subintervals of `I` and `I'`; hence `x` commutes with the whole subnet.
In particular it commutes with the common rotation group. The ambient
vacuum is the unique zero-energy vector, so `x Omega` is scalar, and
the separating vacuum for `N_H(I)` forces `x` to be scalar. This is the
explicit local irreducibility step that should precede the index theorem.

[Kawahigashi--Longo, Proposition 2.3, p. 504](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v160-n2-p04.pdf)
then gives finite index: its assumption is an irreducible local extension
of a net with finitely many irreducible DHR sectors and decomposition
into finite-statistical-dimension sectors. Complete rationality supplies
these assumptions. The subsequent `1,1,2` multiplicities and composition
algebra of endomorphisms refer to this actual inclusion, so the
pointwise automorphism comparison is not being transferred through an
uncontrolled abstract net isomorphism.

## Final sign-involution and net-category check

The revised index-two exclusion is valid without assuming the full
pointwise stabilizer `Aut(B/U)` is trivial. If all six boundary objects
were invertible, the actual net index would be six and the intermediate
index `[N_B:N_U]` would be two. Its sign automorphism lifts to a unitary
VOA automorphism of the actual `B` by
[CKLW, Theorem 6.9](https://arxiv.org/html/1503.01260).
Their Proposition 7.6 identifies the fixed net with the net of the fixed
VOA; Theorem 7.5 then gives `B^g=U`. The current Lie-algebra rank
contradiction in the structure fragment excludes this possibility.
This comparison uses the established smeared-field net construction,
and does not depend on the remaining annulus compatibility A1.

The direct net-category conclusion also bypasses A1. The boundary
category of the actual holomorphic net extension is already a unitary
fusion category. Its Haagerup--Izumi fusion ring and untwisted pointed
subcategory satisfy
[Izumi's classification hypotheses for `Z/3`](https://arxiv.org/html/1609.07604):
the remaining second-cohomology and `G/2G` obstructions vanish.
The two classes are the Morita-equivalent Haagerup categories `H2,H3`,
as in [Grossman--Snyder, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.2631).
Their centers are braided equivalent, their positive spherical
structures give the ribbon structures, and
[Reutter's Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.09710)
makes the center equivalence unitary. Thus the claimed net
representation category is justified conditional on the stated
algebraic/categorical inputs. This does not establish positivity on
the VOA's own intertwining-operator spaces, which is the reason A1
remains relevant to the stronger VOA conclusions.

## Final exact-text audit of the pairwise A1 proof

This review checked the completed proposition, its inverse-domain and
continuation lemmas, and the ensuing complete-unitarity theorem. The
following points explain why the earlier obstruction is resolved.

1. **The geometric incoming operator is identified.** For the semigroup
   generated by `-z rho(z) d/dz`, the framing
   `h(theta,s)=phi_(t-s)(exp(i theta))` has vector field
   `(-h_s/h_theta) d/dtheta = i rho(exp(i theta)) d/dtheta`.
   Its integrated action is `exp(-t L(rho))`; thin factors supply the
   specified boundary reparametrizations. This is the convention of
   [HTAnn, Remark 4.2, formula (5.2), and Theorem 5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08684).
   The nonnegative Fourier modes preserve finite energy cutoffs, so
   the integrated evolution agrees with Tener's finite-energy
   exponential. The case of zero semigroup time uses only thin factors.

2. **Lifts are constructed for the pair first.** Choose the common
   localizer `delta` supplied by
   [Tener, Lemma 3.19 and Corollary 3.20](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08168).
   The localized annuli `delta A_X` and `delta A_Xtilde` admit lifts
   `D_A,D_Atilde` in the identity-component localized extension
   `Bigon_c(I)`, whose fiber is `C^times`, as defined in
   [HTAnn, Section 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08684).
   Normalize their scalar fibers to the prescribed vacuum operators,
   and only then set `A_under=delta_tilde^{-1}D_A` and
   `B_under=D_Atilde^dagger delta_tilde`, using the same lift of the
   prescribed central diffeomorphism. Their product is the localized
   lift `D_Atilde^dagger D_A`. Local transport in HTAnn Theorem 7.4
   gives exactly the module action in Tener Section 5.1, formula (5.2).
   Changing the common deck lift multiplies the factors by opposite
   powers of `exp(2 pi i L0)`; these cancel in the insertion form because
   all VOA modes shift energy by integers. Vacuum scalar normalization
   alone is never used to determine a nonvacuum deck action.

3. **Both incoming-domain conditions hold.** The inner Jordan domain
   contains zero. A small round disc inside it supplies a complementary
   annulus `C`, with its outer parametrization chosen for sewing. Adjust
   its central lift so that `A_M C_M=lambda r^{L0}`. For a finite-energy
   vector `v`, the preimage `C_M lambda^{-1}r^{-L0}v` proves
   `Mfin` is contained in the range of `A_M`. Injectivity gives
   `A_M^{-1}Mfin=C_M Mfin`; boundedness and dense range of `C_M` make
   that inverse domain dense. Thus the proof supplies both conditions
   of Tener Definition 4.2, not just density. The same argument applies
   to the outgoing adjoint. The annulus injectivity and dense-range
   theorem used here is
   [HTNet, Lemma 4.15](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20735v3).

4. **Point insertions agree with the module fields.** The localized
   product with a point insertion gives a bounded local operator and
   its sector image. In the incoming interior, round divisibility is
   exactly the hypothesis needed for HTNet Theorem 9.5(i), and sewing
   gives the desired insertion form. On the outgoing interior, the
   adjoint statement includes the coordinate transform
   `exp(bar(z)L1)(-bar(z)^(-2))^{L0}Theta v`, which is the unitary VOA
   adjoint transform. These steps use
   [HTNet, Lemma 6.8, formula (76), Proposition 6.19 and formula (81)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20735v3).
   Holomorphicity is supplied by Corollary 6.15. Matrix coefficients
   on the two inverse-energy domains are finite Laurent polynomials.
   The identity theorem therefore extends equality over every component
   of the sewn interior, including seam points. Density of both test
   domains gives uniqueness of the bounded extension; no graph-core
   assertion for those domains is needed.

5. **All representatives and all generators are covered.** A single
   geometric realization need not account for the full prescribed
   interior of an operator pair. The final proof repeats the argument
   for every realizing geometric pair and takes the union of their
   sewn interiors, following
   [Tener's proof of Theorem 4.28](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08168).
   The canonical paired form and uniqueness of its bounded extension
   identify the operators on overlaps. Local transport also handles
   the no-insertion operator `BA`, including pairs with empty insertion
   interior. Hence the proof covers every generator of the
   bounded-insertion local algebra.

6. **The vacuum comparison is concrete.** HTNet Theorem 8.9 identifies
   the reconstructed vacuum VOA with the original `U`. The preceding
   insertion calculation therefore proves
   `A_U^bl(I) subset N_U(I)` on the same Hilbert completion of `U`.
   By Tener Proposition 4.13, the bounded-insertion construction is
   already a vacuum conformal net with this cyclic vacuum. Its Haag
   duality and locality of the smeared-field net give
   `A_U^bl(I) subset N_U(I) subset A_U^bl(I')'=A_U^bl(I)`.
   Thus the nets are equal, not merely abstractly isomorphic. The
   common-Hilbert-space hypothesis is essential to this argument.

7. **The positivity endpoint has no extra tensor-comparison premise.**
   Earlier exhaustion supplies a unitary structure and a reconstructed
   sector for every simple `U`-module; finite direct sums preserve this.
   The pairwise identity is exactly the representation requirement in
   [Tener, Fusion and positivity, Definition 2.18](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.08257v3).
   His Corollary 4.6 then applies to the simple regular unitary VOA
   with bounded localized fields and yields the canonical positive
   fusion structure. This step does not invoke a charged-intertwiner
   locality theorem or assume a tensor equivalence from linear full
   faithfulness. Strong unitarity and regularity have already been
   established before this corollary is used.
