# Parafermion identification: a terminating finite reconstruction

This note repairs the proof-transport paragraph in
`hi_cft_20260923_proof_pf13_identification.md`. It is an algebraic audit,
not a new lattice calculation. The existing exact polynomial records
remain the inputs. The reconstruction needed for the actual canonical
first-row OPEs is valid, but an unqualified appeal to increasing total
input weight conceals a step: for example, evaluating
`a_1(a_0 d)` starts with a polynomial of weight seven, so a direct
total-input-weight argument has not yet reduced the pair `(b,d)`.

The following finite schedule removes that circularity. It also avoids
any assumption that the parafermion algebra is freely generated.

## Precise local statement

Let `V` and `V'` be simple nonnegatively graded vertex algebras of CFT
type, generated by a conformal vector `L` and a weight-three primary
`a`. Put

\[
 b=a_1a,\qquad d=a_1b,
 \qquad (\operatorname{wt}L,\operatorname{wt}a,
         \operatorname{wt}b,\operatorname{wt}d)=(2,3,4,5).
\]

Assume that the full singular first rows `a_n a`, `a_n b`, `a_n d`
are identical homogeneous differential normal polynomials in the four
named fields, for every `n >= 0`, with one common choice of parentheses.
For the application the first self-row is

\[
 a_5a=\frac c3\mathbf1,\quad a_4a=0,\quad
 a_3a=2L,\quad a_2a=TL,\quad a_1a=b,\quad
 a_0a=\frac12Tb-\frac1{12}T^3L,
 \tag{1}
\]

and the upper part of the next row is

\[
 a_n b=0\ (n\ge4),\qquad
 a_3b=\bigl(31-16\lambda(2+c)\bigr)a,
 \qquad
 a_2b=\frac83\bigl(5-2\lambda(2+c)\bigr)Ta.
 \tag{2}
\]

Then `V` and `V'` are isomorphic by an isomorphism identifying `L` and
`a` (and consequently `b` and `d`). Here `a_1d` is a specified
weight-six normal polynomial, not an additional independent field.
Both simple poles must also be supplied as polynomials.

The bounded reduction below proves this statement under the explicitly
displayed canonical rows. These are the rows in the saved application;
no stronger general presentation theorem is needed.

## Virasoro factors and the finite list of normal cores

The Virasoro brackets with `b` and `d` are forced by their definitions.
For `m >= 1`, primary covariance gives

\[
 L(m)b=(2m+1)a_{m+1}a,
 \qquad
 L(m)d=(2m+1)a_{m+1}b+a_1L(m)b.
 \tag{3}
\]

Equations (1)--(2) show that these are linear combinations of the
generators and their derivatives: the first expression uses `L`,
while the second uses `a`. Together with the translation and grading
terms, every `L`-generator bracket is therefore known, and it creates
no new nonlinear generator pair.

One may move Virasoro creation operators to the outside of a normal
word using their mode commutators. This leaves sums of Virasoro
creation operators acting on normal products containing only
derivatives of `a,b,d`. Moving only Virasoro modes requires only (3)
and the Virasoro relations. Reassociation gives the same reduction;
its correction brackets also have a Virasoro entry. Derivatives are
handled by sesquilinearity. Up to weight eight there are at most two
non-Virasoro factors, since `3+3+3=9`. The only two-factor cores are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{maximum polynomial weight}&\text{possible cores, besides one field}
\\\hline
6&:aa:\\
7&:aa:,\ :ab:\\
8&:aa:,\ :ab:,\ :bb:,\ :ad:.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

Derivatives on either factor do not add a new core. Either order of
two different factors can be used. In the order displayed, skew
symmetry rewrites the opposite order using their already known
singular bracket. At every stage where (4) is used below, that bracket
has already been supplied or reconstructed.

For clarity, `A_r` below means a common polynomial evaluation of every
`a_n P`, where `n >= 0` and `P` is any differential normal polynomial
of weight at most `r`; `B_r` means the same with `b_n`. Negative modes
that arise while commuting through Virasoro creation operators are
already normal products. The commutator computation reduces the
number of outer Virasoro operators, so it terminates. Thus it suffices
to check the cores in (4).

## A noncircular calculation order

Use the noncommutative Wick identity

\[
 [x_\lambda:yz:]
 =:[x_\lambda y]z:+:y[x_\lambda z]:
    +\int_0^\lambda [[x_\lambda y]_\mu z]\,d\mu.
 \tag{5}
\]

The successive dependencies are as follows.

1. **`A_6`.** The only two-factor core is `:aa:`. In its integral term,
   equation (1) says that `[a_lambda a]` is linear in the vacuum,
   derivatives of `L`, and derivatives of `b`. Its bracket with `a`
   therefore uses only the supplied first rows and Virasoro brackets.

2. **The pair `b,b`.** The iterate identity gives, for every `n >= 0`,

   \[
   b_n s=a_1(a_n s)-a_0(a_{n+1}s)
             +a_{n+1}(a_0s)-a_n(a_1s).
   \tag{6}
   \]

   Set `s=b`. Each inner `a_jb` has weight at most six, and
   `a_1b=d` is already a named generator. Hence `A_6` evaluates every
   term and gives the full `b,b` row.

3. **`A_7`.** The newly possible core is `:ab:`. Its integral term
   brackets derivatives of `L,b` from (1) with `b`. The only additional
   pair is `b,b`, already computed.

4. **The pair `b,d`.** Set `s=d` in (6). The inner `a_jd` has weight
   at most seven. Thus `A_7` computes the whole row. Skew symmetry also
   supplies the reversed row.

5. **`B_6`.** Its only two-factor core is `:aa:`. Each coefficient of
   `[b_lambda a]` is a supplied polynomial of weight at most six.
   Bracketing this with the remaining `a` is an instance of `A_6`,
   after skew symmetry. Single-field inputs use the known `b,b` and
   `b,d` rows as necessary.

6. **`A_8`.** The newly possible cores are `:bb:` and `:ad:`. For
   `:bb:`, the integral term brackets a coefficient of `[a_lambda b]`,
   of weight at most six, with `b`; this is `B_6`. For `:ad:`, equation
   (1) reduces the integral term to the known `L,d` and `b,d` rows.

7. **`B_7`.** The only new core is `:ab:`. Its integral term brackets
   a coefficient of `[b_lambda a]`, of weight at most six, with `b`.
   This is `B_6`, already available.

8. **The pair `d,d`.** Since `d=a_1b`, the iterate identity is

   \[
   d_n s=a_1(b_n s)-a_0(b_{n+1}s)
            +b_{n+1}(a_0s)-b_n(a_1s).
   \tag{7}
   \]

   Put `s=d`. The inner `b_jd` has weight at most eight, so the first
   two terms are evaluated by `A_8`. The inner `a_0d` and `a_1d` have
   weights seven and six, so the other terms are evaluated by `B_7`.

This is a finite acyclic schedule. At every step the identical input
polynomials produce identical output polynomials in the two algebras.
It proves equality of the entire singular OPE table for `L,a,b,d`.
No rank assumption, independence of normal monomials, parity assumption,
or absence of null fields enters this argument. The weight-six
decoupling polynomial and the two simple poles are essential inputs.

## Common simple quotient without a universal-algebra construction

Form the direct product vertex algebra `V x V'`, and let `D` be the
vertex subalgebra generated by the diagonal pairs of the four named
fields. Its vacuum is `(1,1)` and its conformal vector is `(L,L')`.
The OPE calculation above holds identically on these diagonal pairs.
The usual filtered Wick induction therefore shows that their
differential normal span is a vertex subalgebra; since it contains
the generators, it equals `D`. In particular,

\[
 D_n=0\quad(n<0),\qquad D_0=\mathbb C(\mathbf1,\mathbf1).
 \tag{8}
\]

Every proper graded ideal of `D` has zero degree-zero part: otherwise
it contains the vacuum. The sum of all proper graded ideals still
has zero degree-zero part, and is consequently proper. It is therefore
the unique maximal proper graded ideal. Both coordinate projections
`D -> V` and `D -> V'` are surjective, and their kernels are maximal
proper graded ideals because the two target algebras are simple.
Their kernels coincide, giving the required generator-preserving
isomorphism. This argument proves the common-simple-quotient assertion
without needing to construct a freely generated nonlinear vertex
algebra, and without assuming that a specified finite relation set
generates every null relation.

## Application and remaining certificate boundary

For the actual application put `c=8/5`, `lambda=14/473`. The saved
upper-pole record supplies (1)--(2) and the upper `a,d` coefficients.
The nonlinear record supplies `a_0b`. The truncation record supplies
the polynomial `a_1d`. Either the direct weight-seven record or the
separate Jacobi/skew derivation supplies `a_0d`. The Dong--Lam--Yamada
change-of-generators record identifies the seven coefficients of the
weight-six polynomial with the parafermion values. The actual
subalgebra is simple because its generating fields are PCT stable;
the ambient and intrinsic translations agree by the complementary
positive-energy argument. Those membership, PCT, primary, stress,
translation, and exact polynomial assertions remain source-bound
finite inputs, not consequences of the reconstruction lemma.

The primary paper
[Linshaw, equations (5.4), (5.7), and (A.2)](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.02275)
supplies the common first rows in recursive normalization. Its
Section 3.1 also discusses universal vertex algebras with nonlinear
relations and null fields. The direct-product argument above gives
an elementary substitute for that universal construction here.
The Wick and iterate identities used in the proof are standard vertex
algebra identities; see
[De Sole--Kac, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/math-ph/0312042).

The finite identification is thus justified once the listed exact
records and the independent parafermion comparison are accepted. This
does not by itself discharge every part of R1: the embedded Potts
factor, actual inclusion in `U`, positive conformal-vector identity,
and normalization/translation data retain their own input records.
