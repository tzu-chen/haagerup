Summary
=======
The displayed Ising blocks, crossing matrix, monodromy phases, pairing, and
normalized OPE coefficient are consistent. The defect eigenvalues, invariant
projector, twining/twisting transformation, restriction multiplicities,
Haagerup line matrices, self-annulus, and boundary entropy normalization also

rather than incorrect numerical formulas. The glossary definition of a

1. Ising normalization and crossing: checked, with two useful completions
=======================================================================

1.1 r08_sewing.tex:48--63, 97--104 -- define the correlator and its two-point
normalizations explicitly.
The formulas are correct for

    G(z,zbar)=<sigma(infinity) sigma(1) sigma(z,zbar) sigma(0)>,
    <sigma(z,zbar)sigma(0)>=|z|^(-1/4),
    <epsilon(z,zbar)epsilon(0)>=|z|^(-2),

with sigma(infinity)=lim_{|w|->infinity}|w|^(1/4)sigma(w,wbar).
The full sigma has (h,hbar)=(1/16,1/16); full epsilon has (1/2,1/2).
The chiral epsilon label denotes the weight-one-half representation. State
these conventions before the radical formulas. Without the insertion
positions and two-point normalizations, the quoted absolute OPE coefficient
is not intrinsically fixed, even though the text calls it normalized.

Independent expansion gives

    F_1=z^(-1/8)(1+z^2/64+...),
    F_epsilon=(1/2)z^(3/8)(1+z/4+...).

Thus the absence of a linear vacuum correction, both exponents, and the
energy coefficient 1/4 in |F_epsilon|^2 are correct. In the stated unit
two-point normalization, |C_sigma,sigma,epsilon|=1/2 follows.

1.2 r08:66--73 -- the missing BPZ equation can be supplied in two lines.
The null vector (L_-2-(4/3)L_-1^2)|sigma> is correct. For the fixed-position
chiral correlator f(z), Ward identities yield

    (4/3) f'' = (2z-1)/(z(1-z)) f' + h/[z^2(1-z)^2] f,
    h=1/16,

or equivalently

    f'' + 3(1-2z)/(4z(1-z)) f'
        - 3/(64z^2(1-z)^2) f = 0.

Here the derivatives at the insertions 0 and 1 become respectively
(z-1)f'+2hf and -zf'-2hf by global Ward identities. These substitutions
explain the ODE without introducing additional machinery. Its indicial
equation is r(r-1)+(3/4)r-3/64=0, with roots -1/8 and 3/8.
This is a high-value addition: the current paragraph says an ODE exists,
but a textbook reader can readily follow the actual equation.

1.3 r08:75--104 -- crossing and monodromy check.
On 0<z<1 write z=sin^2(theta), 0<theta<pi/2. After removing the common
[z(1-z)]^(-1/8), the two blocks are cos(theta/2) and sin(theta/2).
Under z->1-z, theta->pi/2-theta. The ordinary sine/cosine angle formulas
give exactly the displayed Hadamard matrix, including its signs.

The local powers give monodromy diag(exp(-pi i/4),exp(3pi i/4)); its two
eigenvalues have ratio -1, so every off-diagonal entry of the Hermitian
pairing is excluded. F^t diag(a,b) F has off-diagonal entry (a-b)/2,
forcing D=aI; positivity and vacuum normalization give D=I. These steps
are correct. For a general complex fusing matrix and the convention
G=F_vector^t D conjugate(F_vector), the invariance equation is
M^t D conjugate(M)=D; the simpler printed F^tDF works because this F is real.
No repair is needed for the Ising calculation itself.

1.4 A branch-free closed form also verifies the other crossing generator.
The final result can be written

    G(z,zbar)=sqrt(1+|z|+|1-z|)
                  /[sqrt(2) |z(1-z)|^(1/4)].

Indeed, with w=sqrt(1-z),
(|1+w|+|1-w|)^2=2(1+|1-z|+|z|). This formula makes single-valuedness
manifest and shows both

    G(1-z,1-zbar)=G(z,zbar),
    G(z,zbar)=|z|^(-1/4) G(1/z,1/zbar).

The second equation includes the external-field conformal prefactor. The
chapter currently checks the first generator explicitly; adding this
formula completes a genuinely elementary crossing example and prevents
readers from forgetting such prefactors in other normalizations.

2. Defect categories: state the opposite convention and preservation scope
========================================================================

2.1 r07_defects.tex:193--202 -- define C^op here or in the glossary.
In C boxtimes C^op, 'op' reverses tensor order, not the arrows of the
underlying category. This differs from 'rev' on a braided category, which
reverses the braiding and twist. Neither notation is currently defined in
r09, and they appear in adjacent chapters.

A short derivation is available from the original analytic argument.
For T_(x,y)(m)=x tensor m tensor y,

    T_(x,y) T_(x',y')(m)
      = (x tensor x') tensor m tensor (y' tensor y).

Thus (x,y) tensor (x',y')=(xx',y'y), explaining the reversed right order.
The canonical-algebra double-centralizer theorem identifies these
Z(C)-module endofunctors with A-bimodules. The first-factor embedding is
x->(x,1). This is both a useful proof sketch of the 36 count and a concrete
definition of the convention. Source: hi_cft_c8_manuscript_analytic.tex:
947--964.

2.2 r07:185--202 and r09:88--89 -- keep the chiral preservation hypothesis
attached to the 36-object count.
These are the A-A bimodule defects for the selected full rational extension,
preserving the specified U chiral structure (on the corresponding two sides),
not a classification of every possible E8 defect or every defect preserving
only the stress tensor. In particular they need not preserve the enlarged
E8 current algebra. The chapter already states the last distinction well;
one sentence identifying the counted class as U-linear rational defects
would settle the scope. Also retain the selected H' embedding: equality of
the abstract holomorphic VOA with E8 does not identify it with the original
H/U extension. The current source conventions correctly maintain that point.

2.3 r07:10--11, 38--46, 185--240 -- distinguish the two uses of twelve.
There are twelve Verlinde lines in the diagonal U full theory. The selected
six-line Haagerup action on the E8 full theory has twelve center/tube charges.
These are both indexed by Rep(U)=Z(H3), but their physical Hilbert spaces
are different: M_i tensor conjugate(M_i) versus
M_i tensor conjugate(H'). The chapter explains both constructions correctly,
but one explicit comparison sentence before the extension section would
prevent a beginner from identifying their sector partition functions.

2.4 r07:213--224 -- the restriction and twisted-space identifications are
correct under the canonical extension/defect construction.
Free--forgetful adjunction gives Hom_U(M_mu,Rx)=Hom_C(F(M_mu),x), hence
the stated multiplicity decomposition. The physical identification of the
first-factor twisted space with Rx tensor conjugate(H') additionally uses
the rational defect construction; it does not follow merely from equality
of modular transformation matrices. This hypothesis is present implicitly
through the preceding section and could be recalled in the introducing
sentence, as the original physics source does.

2.5 r07:287--299 -- the annulus formula has the correct dual and normalization.
An illuminating one-line derivation is

    Hom_C(F(M_mu) tensor x,y)
       = Hom_C(F(M_mu),x* tensor y)
       = Hom_U(M_mu,R(x* tensor_A y)),

where the central half-braiding moves F(M_mu) past x as required. This
explains why x* must occur, rather than importing it from a commutative
Verlinde ring. Since dim_U Rz=dim(A) dim_C z and S00 dim(A)=1, the closed
vacuum coefficient is d_x d_y, giving the printed positive g_x=d_x.
The self-annulus coefficients (1,4,5,3,3,3,3,...,3), its nine weight-1/3
states, and the invertible self-annulus chi_E8 agree with the fusion rules.

3. Sewing algebra hypotheses and the two different associators
============================================================

3.1 r08_sewing.tex:41--44 -- explicitly distinguish chiral-block F from
the six-line H3 F.
For U conformal blocks the fusing matrices belong to Rep(U)=Z(H3).
For junctions of the selected six symmetry lines they belong to H3 itself.
These are related by the center construction, but they are not the same
matrices: in particular the 4x4 rho recoupling block in f02 is not a
four-point conformal-block matrix for arbitrary U primaries. This sentence
would connect the prerequisites to the main construction at precisely the
point where readers are most likely to overidentify the two meanings of F.

3.2 r08:115--122 -- an abstract Frobenius coproduct is not automatically
an adjoint.
The symmetric special Frobenius framework applies to a modular tensor
category attached to an existing rational chiral theory and organizes its
oriented sewing data. Frobenius relates multiplication to a compatible
comultiplication. Only in the chosen positive dagger/Q-system structure may
one identify that comultiplication with the adjoint, up to normalization.
The current phrase 'its adjoint splitting' runs those two levels together.

Repair: write 'its compatible comultiplication; in the unitary Q-system
normalization this is the adjoint of multiplication'. State once that the
chiral theory and its conformal blocks are already supplied: an abstract
modular category plus a Frobenius algebra does not construct the analytic
chiral CFT from scratch. In this book those chiral and positivity hypotheses
are supplied by the preceding construction and analytic chapters.

The boundary/module and defect/bimodule dictionary matches the primary FRS
framework directly checked at https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0204148.
The orientation qualifier matters: unoriented surfaces need extra reversion
data (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0511590). No need to develop that extra
theory; simply say 'oriented' when first stating the framework.

3.3 r08:131--134 -- commutative and twist-free alone are not the full
extension hypotheses.
Recall connectedness and separability, and the positive Q-system structure
for a unitary extension. The earlier chapters establish all of these for
the chosen canonical algebra, so this is a wording repair, not an additional
unproved input. Suggested phrase: 'a connected commutative separable
twist-free algebra, with the positive structure and grading conditions of
the extension theorem, supplies the additional chiral fields.'

3.4 r08:124--131 -- clarify the right-moving dual convention.
The canonical object sum_i M_i boxtimes M_i* is standard. Its relation to
the physical notation sum_i M_i tensor conjugate(M_i) depends on the chosen
contragredient/antiholomorphic dictionary. For all twelve Haagerup labels
and for Ising, every label is self-dual, so no formula changes. If this
paragraph is meant generally, say which right-moving convention makes this
the diagonal rather than charge-conjugation matrix. This is a convention
clarification; I found no contradiction in the actual self-dual examples.

3.5 r08:185--198 -- attach the branching assumptions to the displayed
factorized character identity.
For the equality chi_i^V=sum chi_lambda^A b_i,lambda one needs a genuine
semisimple restriction as an A-module, compatible ordinary gradings, and
the complementary conformal-vector relation omega_V=omega_A+omega_C.
Commuting fields by themselves do not imply that additive L0 relation or
discrete factorized character identity. The paragraph says a module 'can'
branch this way and later flags convergence, which is appropriately cautious;
putting the conformal-vector relation in the initial sentence makes the
displayed equality precise. Retain the later warning about infinite sums,
vacuum multiplicity, and sewing: those are correctly separated.

3.6 r08:237--245 -- remove the unnecessary word 'commuting'.
The ordinary Haagerup line algebra is noncommutative. Two commuting
operators can represent its scalar quotient, so the example is not literally
false, but that choice distracts from the intended point. Say 'operators
may obey the fusion-algebra relations on a vector space while carrying no
junction associator data'. If only the rho-square relation is printed,
call it one of the relations rather than suggesting it is the full ring.

4. Glossary corrections and refinements
======================================

4.1 r09_notation.tex:83--87 -- all-module tensor products need hypotheses.
C_A is a category of right modules for any algebra object. It is not in
general monoidal merely because C is braided. In the setting used here,
A is connected commutative separable; the braiding equips a right module
with a compatible left action, and balancing gives tensor_A. Add those
adjectives. For local modules, the precise condition is invariance of the
action under monodromy, r c_(A,X)c_(X,A)=r. This is an equation involving
the action; it does not require the underlying double braiding itself to
be the identity morphism on all of X tensor A. Do not conflate the two.

4.2 r09:215--218 -- primitive transfer definition needs simplicity.
'A unique peripheral eigenvalue' can mean only one distinct eigenvalue
value. The identity map on M_2(C) has only the eigenvalue 1, but is not
primitive. State that the normalized peripheral eigenvalue 1 is simple,
all other eigenvalues have modulus less than one, and the left/right fixed
metrics are strictly positive on the specified supported algebra. Equivalently
for the finite completely positive setting, an appropriate positive power
sends every nonzero positive operator to a strictly positive one.
The parallel statement in l04_tensor_networks.tex:169 also merits the word
'simple'; I sent that small point to its author.

4.3 r09:237--240 -- tube factors really do multiply physical degeneracies.
Replace 'central charge sectors' by 'center charge sectors' to avoid the
Virasoro-central-charge meaning. Also qualify 'not a CFT state multiplicity':
dim W_x^mu is not the dimension or level degeneracy of the dynamical space
V_mu, but it is the number of copies of that dynamical space in H_x and
multiplies its level degeneracies. The current absolute wording conflicts
with the correct factor-two discussion at r07:226--229.

4.4 r09:208--209 -- ordinary modules can be sums of lowest-weight strings.
For a simple ordinary module, a single lowest-weight grading is correct.
For a general ordinary module the precise definition is semisimple L0,
finite-dimensional eigenspaces, and lower truncation along each integral
weight string. A finite direct sum can have several h+Z strings. The glossary
can say 'lower-truncated weight grading' or explicitly restrict its entry
to simple ordinary modules. This is a small precision point.

4.5 Add the conventions C^op versus C^rev noted in item 2.1, and mention D
as the local notation for intrinsic translation used in the construction
chapters. The current mathsf T entry correctly distinguishes translation
from a stress field and modular T, but D occurs often enough to deserve
the cross-reference.

5. Additional short worked examples with high pedagogical value
=============================================================

5.1 Recover Haagerup twisted characters from the twined character vectors.
In the center ordering (0,b,a,c0,c1,c2,d1,...,d6), define

    t_alpha=(1,1,-1,0,...,0),
    t_rho=(d,-1/d,0,...,0).

Direct multiplication by the explicit S in f04 gives

    S t_alpha=(0,0,0,1,1,1,0,...,0)=n_alpha,
    S t_rho=(0,1,1,1,1,1,1,...,1)=n_rho.

For the second vector the nonresidue rows use
(d+1/d)/sqrt(13)=1, while the four middle rows use
(d-1/d)/3=1. The first row vanishes because S_0b=d^2 S_00.
This makes the relation among the small fusion-algebra matrices, modular
transformation, and actual restriction multiplicities transparent. No large
Verlinde computation is required.

5.2 Derive one twisted scalar multiplicity instead of quoting its fusion
integer. With the f04 abbreviations A=S00, B=S0b, u=1/3 and AB=1/117,

    N_(c1,c1)^(c1)=(1/27)(1/A+1/B)+5/9=13/9+5/9=2.

The second term is the sum of cubes of a middle-block row divided by u;
its entries are one 2/3 and three -1/3. The c1 top has dimension three,
so the scalar ground multiplicity is 2*3^2=18. The defect ground itself
comes instead from the two vacuum/c1 placements and has multiplicity six.
This extends r07's good ground-versus-scalar example by the one missing
fusion computation.

5.3 A small condensation model for all modules versus local modules.
The Z3 center already worked out in f03 has labels (g,k) and monodromy
exp(2pi i(kg'+k'g)/3). Take A=direct_sum_k(0,k). The three right modules
x_g=direct_sum_k(g,k) satisfy x_g tensor_A x_h=x_(g+h). Thus all modules
form Vec_Z3. Locality with every condensed charge forces g=0, leaving only
the vacuum local module. Twisting the left A action gives nine bimodules.
This reproduces, entirely by finite characters, the distinction among a
trivial local-module category, a nontrivial all-module category, and a larger
bimodule category. It is the cleanest elementary precursor to the 6/12/36
Haagerup discussion.

5.4 Distinct noninvertible boundaries have twelve light boundary fields.
The exercise is well chosen; a selected solution is a one-line ring
calculation: rho*(alpha rho)=alpha^2 rho^2=alpha^2+rho+alpha rho+alpha^2 rho.
After restriction the coefficient of chi_c1 is 1+3=4, hence 4*3=12
weight-1/3 fields, compared with nine for the self-annulus. The extra three
come from a nontrivial invertible channel, not an unexplained degeneracy.

Suggested revision order: glossary primitivity and tensor-product hypotheses;
the op/rev convention and the two F-matrix meanings; explicit Ising insertion
and normalization; then the BPZ/branch-free example and twined-to-twisted
matrix check. The existing distinction between a sewn full theory and a
chiral commutant is mathematically sound and should remain prominent.
