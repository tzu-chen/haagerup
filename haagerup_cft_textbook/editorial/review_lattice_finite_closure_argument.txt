1. Filtered Wick induction: c02:88–106

The relevant ordering is total INPUT weight, not the weight of the
eventual OPE coefficient. This is used consistently.

Let w=wt(a)+wt(b)+wt(c) for [a_lambda :bc:]. Both normal factors b,c
must have positive weight. Then [a_lambda b] and [a_lambda c] involve
strictly smaller total input weights. By the lower-weight induction,
their coefficients already belong to the homogeneous normal span.

In the Wick integral, a coefficient a_(j)b has weight
wt(a)+wt(b)-j-1. Thus its bracket with c has total input weight
w-j-1 <= w-1, so the same induction applies after expressing that
coefficient homogeneously in N. If its weight is zero, its known
membership in N makes it a scalar vacuum; the bracket vanishes.
Negative-weight coefficients cannot occur nontrivially in N.

This remains valid if both inputs are composite. Skew symmetry first
moves a selected composite to the right; the formal substitution
-lambda-D only differentiates coefficients already known to be in N.
For a derivative, sesquilinearity reduces the underlying pair's total
weight and then restores only formal lambda factors or D on coefficients.
The vacuum factor is harmless.

There is therefore no hidden assumption that a_(j)b is itself a
generator, or that a chosen normal-word list is independent. Arbitrary
parenthesizations in the definition of N are doing useful work.

2. Recursive theorem: c02:125–177

The proof first establishes a_(0)N subset N for every a in A. This
step needs only the stated generator memberships, the zero-mode
derivation identity, a_(0)1=0, and [D,a_(0)]=0. It does not use general
singular closure and is correctly placed before the pair induction.

For a generator pair (s,t), put d=wt(s), e=wt(t). In the non-base
case d>=3, recursive provenance replaces s by a_(0)b plus homogeneous
normal remainders, with wt(b)=d-1. A remainder made from generators of
weights <d cannot be a single undifferentiated generator of weight d.
Every nonzero remainder is therefore a derivative or a positive-weight
composite and is covered by the filtered lemma at total weight d+e.

The Jacobi identity
  (a_(0)b)_(n)t=a_(0)(b_(n)t)-b_(n)(a_(0)t)
is valid for every n>=0, including n=0.
- b_(n)t is a strictly lower-total-weight pair; a_(0) then preserves N.
- a_(0)t is already a known element of N of weight e+1.
- Derivative/composite terms in that expression again use only pairs
  with smaller total input weight.
- The only remaining term is a single generator of weight e+1.
  Paired with b, it has the original total weight d+e but left weight
  d-1, strictly earlier in the lexicographic order.
- If e=h, no generator of weight h+1 exists, so only the reducible
  normal terms remain.

Thus the ordering (total input weight,left input weight) terminates.
No unstated symmetry of the ordered generator pairs is needed.
Left weights1 and2 are exactly the first hypothesis.

For the actual h=4 list, the nontrivial cases can be seen explicitly:
(3,3) can reach (2,4), which is a base case; (4,3) can reach (3,4),
which is earlier; a right input of weight4 produces only normal
expressions of weight5, since there is no fifth strong-generator space.
All smaller-right-weight cases reduce in the same way.

After generator closure is established, ordinary total-weight Wick
induction handles all normal expressions. The negative-mode identity
  x_(-r)y=:(D^(r-1)x)y:/(r-1)!
has the correct factorial and sign, and closes the remaining modes.

The finite-range statement at :179–183 is correct. Positive grading
also bounds the otherwise displayed n>=0 range: for A with S_d,
only n<=d+1 can be nonzero. At each fixed output weight the normal
span has finitely many recipes in the finite positive-weight spaces.
