4. A genuine foundational gap worth filling
-------------------------------------------

The book repeatedly needs the distinction between an invertible fusion
group and its associator class: the untwisted Vec_Z3 in c05, possible
gauging anomalies in l06, and why an algebra-object sum may fail to
multiply. f03 currently mentions a three-cocycle but gives no cocycle
formula or concrete anomalous example. A half-page worked Vec_Z2 example
would connect these ideas without a group-cohomology prerequisite.

Let g^2=1 and let alpha_{a,b,c}=omega(a,b,c)=(-1)^(abc), a,b,c in{0,1}.
The pentagon is
 omega(b,c,d) omega(a,b+c,d) omega(a,b,c)
 =omega(a,b,c+d) omega(a+b,c,d),
and both exponents expand to abc+abd+acd+bcd modulo2. Thus this is a
valid unitary associator with the same two-object fusion ring as Vec_Z2.
Its only nontrivial value is omega(1,1,1)=-1. A normalized change of
binary-junction basis cannot change that value: the relevant 2-cochain
ratio is u(1,1)u(0,1)/[u(1,1)u(1,0)]=1.

Try to condense A=1+g with unit-normalized multiplication and nonzero
g*g->1 coefficient beta. Associativity on g tensor g tensor g equates
beta with -beta, impossible over C. Setting beta=0 leaves a degenerate
nonseparable algebra and does not give the required condensation. This
is an elementary illustration of a gauging obstruction carried by the
associator. It does not imply that the actual H3 order-three subgroup is
anomalous; c05 separately proves that its associator is untwisted.

If root approves, this fits naturally after the existing algebra-object
example in f03. No need to introduce the whole machinery of group
cohomology or claim that this exhausts every gauging problem.

Follow-up: root authorized this example, and it is now in f03. It includes
the nonsplitting argument for the beta=0 right regular module, so failure
of separability is explained rather than only asserted.
