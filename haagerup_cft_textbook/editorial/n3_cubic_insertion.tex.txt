\subsection{Completing the order-three cubics by hand}

At order three we can now forget the special-function origin and solve
the finite coefficient problem directly. Choose either root of
$\delta^2=3\delta+1$, and choose $p,q$ with $p+q=1$ and $pq=\delta$.
The displayed matrix then satisfies rotation, the axis sum, and the
quadratics by the elementary checks above. We will verify all its
cubics using only these same three relations. Identifying these
algebraic numbers with the gamma samples is the separate analytic
step already discussed.

Put $\tau=\delta-1$, $a=\delta-2$, and $\kappa=7-\delta$.
Useful consequences of the three relations are
\[
 p^2=p-\delta,\quad q^2=q-\delta,\quad
 \tau^2=\delta+2,\quad a\tau=3,\quad a^2=5-\delta,
 \quad\frac{\tau^3}{\delta}=\kappa.
\]
Neither denominator vanishes: neither zero nor one solves the
dimension equation. After multiplying \eqref{eq:HI-cubic} by
$\tau^3$, define its left side and its quadratic product by
\[
 \begin{split}
 C_{g,h}(k,l)&=\sum_{m=0}^2
       \mathsf B_{m,g+h}\mathsf B_{g,m+k}\mathsf B_{h,m+l},\\
 R_{g,h}(k,l)&=\mathsf B_{g+l,k}\mathsf B_{h+k,l}.
 \end{split}
\]
All indices are modulo three. The required equality is
\[
 C_{g,h}(k,l)=\tau R_{g,h}(k,l)
                    -\kappa\mathbf1_{g=0}\mathbf1_{h=0}.
\]
When $g=h=0$, the correction is present for every $k,l$, not only
for $k=l=0$.

Three entries show the different cancellations. At $(g,h,k,l)=(1,1,0,0)$,
the three summands are $-1,p,-p^2$, giving
\[
 C_{1,1}(0,0)=-1+p-p^2=\delta-1=\tau R_{1,1}(0,0).
\]
At $(1,1,1,1)$, the equality is $p^3-2=\tau q^2$:
\[
 p^3-2=-\tau p-\delta-2=-\tau p-\tau^2,
 \qquad
 \tau q^2=\tau(1-p-\delta)=-\tau p-\tau^2.
\]
At the exceptional all-zero entry, the full residual is
\[
 a^3-2-\tau a^2+\kappa=-a^2+5-\delta=0.
\]
The invertible-channel correction is essential in this last check.

Here is a complete finite verification, rather than an inference
from these three examples. Regard $C_{g,h}$ and $R_{g,h}$ as
$3\times3$ matrices with row $k$ and column $l$, in the order
$0,1,2$. Write $b=-a^2+a-1$ to shorten one table. Expanding the
three-term sums gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 (g,h)&C_{g,h}&R_{g,h}\\\hline
 (0,0)&
 \begin{pmatrix}a^3-2&b&b\\b&b&3a\\b&3a&b\end{pmatrix}&
 \begin{pmatrix}a^2&1&1\\1&1&\delta\\1&\delta&1\end{pmatrix}
 \\[5pt]
 (0,1)&
 \begin{pmatrix}-3&\tau&-\tau p\\
       \tau&-\tau q&-\tau q\\-3&-\tau p&\tau\end{pmatrix}&
 \begin{pmatrix}-a&1&-p\\1&-q&-q\\-a&-p&1\end{pmatrix}
 \\[5pt]
 (1,1)&
 \begin{pmatrix}\tau&\tau&3p\\
       \tau&p^3-2&\tau\\3p&\tau&\tau\end{pmatrix}&
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1&ap\\1&q^2&1\\ap&1&1\end{pmatrix}
 \\[5pt]
 (1,2)&
 \begin{pmatrix}\tau&-\tau q&-3\\
       -3&-\tau q&\tau\\-\tau p&\tau&-\tau p\end{pmatrix}&
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-q&-a\\-a&-q&1\\-p&1&-p\end{pmatrix}
\end{array}
\]
The entries already use $p+q=1$ and $pq=\delta$.
For instance, a mixed entry reduces as
$-ap-1+q=-\tau p$, and $a\delta-2=\tau$.
The first row of tables obeys $C_{0,0}=\tau R_{0,0}-\kappa\mathbf J$,
where $\mathbf J$ is the all-ones matrix: besides the all-zero
entry checked above,
\[
 b=2\delta-8=\tau-\kappa,
 \qquad3a=3\delta-6=\tau\delta-\kappa.
\]
The other three rows obey $C=\tau R$ by $a\tau=3$ and
$p^3-2=\tau q^2$.

These four tables contain $36$ checked entries. Two symmetries
cover the other ordered indices. Interchanging $(g,k)$ with $(h,l)$
only exchanges commuting scalar factors on both sides. Simultaneously
negating all four indices exchanges $p$ and $q$, because
\[
 \mathsf B_{-r,-s}(p,q)=\mathsf B_{r,s}(q,p).
\]
The scalar relations are unchanged by that exchange. The ordered
pairs $(g,h)$ therefore have four classes,
\[
 \{(0,0)\},\quad
 \{(0,1),(1,0),(0,2),(2,0)\},\quad
 \{(1,1),(2,2)\},\quad
 \{(1,2),(2,1)\}.
\]
Each class has a representative in the table, with all nine choices
of $k,l$. Thus all $81$ cubic equations hold, including every
exceptional or repeated index.

This is an independent elementary solution of the order-three
coefficient equations. At the positive embedding $\delta=d$, its
two roots are
\[
 p,q=\frac{1\pm i\sqrt{4d-1}}2.
\]
They are complex conjugates, so the displayed $\mathsf A$ is
Hermitian. That matrix property alone does not prove unitarity of
the complete reconstructed associator or identify it with the
physical $\HH_3$ associator. General odd order still requires
the analytic coefficient theorem, and categorical reconstruction
remains its own structural step.
