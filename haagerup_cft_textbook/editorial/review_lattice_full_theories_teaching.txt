Line numbers refer to the versions read during this pass.

1. r05:47-57, physical meaning of the extension pairing.
   A concrete two-sentence bridge would prevent a reader from treating
   extension as a relabelling of the diagonal theory's existing states:

   "For example, the diagonal c_1 tensor bar(c_1) sector supplies nine
   scalars of dimension 2/3, whereas its coefficient is zero in the
   extension partition function. Conversely, the extended theory contains
   four copies of a tensor bar(a), because there are two independent
   copies of a on each chiral side."

   The character coefficient v_i v_j already implies this; the point is
   to exhibit actual states being selected and multiplied before the
   reader enters the larger multiplicity tables.

2. r05:237-244, why the local abelian family has r^2 parameters.
   The boost exercise checks a chosen off-diagonal generator but the
   quotient dimension is stated. Optional compact derivation:

   "Writing an infinitesimal O(r,r) generator in blocks gives
   X=(A,B;B^t,D), with A and D antisymmetric. A and D are independent
   rotations of the two current bases; after quotienting these out the
   arbitrary real r by r matrix B remains, giving r^2 directions."

   This makes the parameter count follow from the same matrix algebra
   as the exercise, without adding another theorem or global-moduli
   assertion.

3. r07:178-186, derive the annulus normalization rather than memorize it.
   The formulas are correct. A short geometric explanation would help:

   "The open interval can be unfolded to a chiral circle of length 2 ell,
   which gives H_open=(pi v/ell)(L_0-c/24). In the closed channel the
   spatial circle has circumference v beta, and propagation through
   distance ell gives exp[-2 pi ell/(v beta)
   (L_0+bar L_0-c/12)]. This is exactly the Ishibashi regulator above
   with tilde q=exp[-4 pi ell/(v beta)]."

   This supplies the otherwise implicit factor-of-two reasoning. It
   also makes explicit why the open trace contains one character, not
   an absolute square.

4. r07:388-391, the finite tube factor versus physical state counting.
   The existing distinction is excellent; one formula would make it
   directly usable:

   "At left grade k and right grade ell in a specified mu summand,
   the state multiplicity is n_{x mu} a_mu(k) a_E8(ell).
   Thus n_{1a}=2 and a_a(0)=81 give 162 holomorphic weight-one
   states against the antiholomorphic vacuum."

   This explicitly separates the small line matrices from a truncated
   dynamical Hilbert space. The alpha-twist solution now gives the other
   simple example 1 times 3 times 1 = 3.

5. r07:481-492, a quick orientation consistency check.
   Optional addition after the Hom-space argument:

   "The identity multiplicity is dim Hom(x,y)=delta_{xy} for elementary
   boundaries. For example, the alpha self-annulus uses
   alpha^* alpha=1; omitting the dual would incorrectly use alpha^2
   and lose the boundary identity."

   This is a particularly useful check because the noninvertible
   boundaries used in the worked example are self-dual and by
   themselves cannot expose a mistaken missing dual.
