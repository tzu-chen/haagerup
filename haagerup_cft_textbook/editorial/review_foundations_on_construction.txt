1. Corrections and necessary clarifications
=========================================

1.1 c01_lattice_embedding.tex:45--57 -- cocycle diagonal term.
The chapter starts with an arbitrary positive even lattice and an integral
basis. In that generality the displayed epsilon(a,b) is not guaranteed to
satisfy epsilon(a,-a)=(-1)^((a,a)/2). Its diagonal exponent must be

    sum_i (G_ii/2) a_i b_i + sum_{i>j} a_i b_j G_ij   (mod 2).

Counterexample to the current statement: a rank-one even lattice with a
generator of norm four. The printed formula gives epsilon(alpha,-alpha)=-1,
whereas (-1)^((alpha,alpha)/2)=+1. The later E6+A2 simple-root basis has
G_ii=2, so the actual construction is unaffected.

Repair: use the general formula, then say that its first sum simplifies to
sum_i a_i b_i for the norm-two root basis used below. Also replace the eight-
element basis by a rank-r basis in this introductory general paragraph, or
say explicitly here that the discussion is now specialized to rank eight.
This supplies a useful exercise: verify bilinearity, the commutator sign,
and the diagonal sign for the corrected formula.

1.2 c02_finite_closure.tex:244 -- TeX typo.
The map defining f_a has ',qquad' rather than ',\\qquad'.

1.3 c03_factors.tex:363 -- ambiguous claim of a finite-field calculation.
The phrase 'The finite field calculation gives' precedes exact rational
projection coefficients. In this context it reads as a finite-field
certificate, which by itself would not establish those characteristic-zero
equalities. The source argument is an exact calculation in the finite-

Repair: write 'The exact calculation in this finite-dimensional space gives'.
The nearby construction-input box already scopes the equality appropriately.

1.4 c05_branching.tex:84--99 -- the per-core W is bookkeeping, not an
individual ambient-energy trace.
The coefficient 83/6084 is correct: (3403/4563)(3/164)=83/6084. But an
individual A_L-module top need not be a complete right sl2 representation.
Consequently replacing the right Sugawara trace by 3Q/164 is valid after
summing on E, which is a complete right sl2 representation; it is not
necessarily valid separately on each core top. The current final warning
about individual Cartan eigenvectors is helpful but does not quite state this
distinction.

Repair: define W for each core top as an additive charge-based bookkeeping
quantity. State explicitly that the sum W_total equals Tr_E L_0 only after
assembling a complete U-module top E. The individual W entries need not be
ambient conformal energies or energy traces on invariant U-subspaces. This
also explains why W/dim can exceed the final bound for an isolated table row.

1.5 c05_branching.tex:357--361 -- exclude a trivial right action before
calling it the same cycle or its inverse.
The centralizer of a three-cycle in S_3 has three elements, including the
identity. Commuting with the free left action alone does not eliminate that
identity. Duality does: it conjugates the left and right invertible actions,
so the right action is also transitive.

Repair: insert that sentence, then retain the current commutativity argument.
For self-duality, add the two-line calculation. If rho*=a^k rho and
rho a=a^2 rho, then (a^i rho)*=a^{i+k}rho. Applying duality twice gives
2k=0 modulo three, hence k=0. All three noninvertibles are self-dual.

1.6 c05_branching.tex:393--400 -- restore the arithmetic hypotheses for
the totient bound.
The bound phi(2D)/sqrt(D)>=12/sqrt(13), except D=10, is false for general
squarefree D; D=2,5,7 are immediate counterexamples. It is correct for the
special squareclasses arising here. The source includes the essential
restrictions that the chapter has omitted:

    Delta=9m^2+4=t^2 D, D squarefree;
    (3m)^2 < Delta < (3m+1)^2, hence D>1;
    Delta=1 mod 3, hence 3 does not divide t and D=1 mod 3;
    7 does not divide Delta, since m^2=5 mod 7 has no solution.

For odd D, a single prime is therefore at least 13; the first possible pair
is bounded below by the contribution of 5 and 11. Extra prime factors
increase the product. For even D, phi(2D)/sqrt(D) has an extra sqrt(2), and
the single odd-prime case is D=10 or has prime at least 11. These observations
give exactly the printed inequality. Put them before its use.

The root-sum lower bound also deserves its precise status: it is a specialized
theorem about expressing quadratic irrationalities as sums of roots of unity,
not a direct triangle inequality. Name that theorem and explain that applying
it to two copies of the multiset clears the half-integral coefficients.
Source: hi_cft_c8_manuscript_structure.tex:1295--1317.

1.7 c05_branching.tex:420--423 -- parity and the exceptional inequality.
State why m,t are even when Delta=10t^2, so m=2 is the only possible positive
case below four. Then give the short estimate that currently disappears in
'the bound is again too large':

    N >= m^2/2 + 4mt,
    t/m = sqrt((9+4/m^2)/10) > sqrt(9/10) > 15/16,
    m^2/2 + 4mt > 17m^2/4 > 4m^2+2  for m>=4.

The m=t=2 second-moment contradiction is correctly stated. The extra
hypothesis that the squared-root sum lies in Q(sqrt(10)) is indispensable and
is correctly retained in the chapter; please do not simplify it away.

2. Finite closure: sound induction, but restore its concrete hypotheses
=====================================================================

2.1 c02_finite_closure.tex:87--170 -- positive assessment.
The filtered Wick reduction and lexicographic induction are correct. In the
Wick integral a coefficient a_(j)b lowers the total input weight by j+1.
Positive weights of the omitted normal factors matter. The unique possible
equal-total-weight term in the recursive Jacobi step is a primitive generator
of weight wt(t)+1 paired with b, whose left weight is one smaller. The
separate proof that a_(0) preserves the normal span prevents circularity.
Neither independence of normal words nor a PBW theorem is used. This is one
of the strongest pedagogical sections in the current draft.

2.2 c02_finite_closure.tex:316--329 and 398--400 -- make the positive-mode
bootstrap visible enough to check the recursive theorem's first hypothesis.
Currently 'repeating with L_1 and L_2' hides several different controls, and
the final application does not display the positive modes on P_4 recovered
after Gate 5. A beginner cannot audit that every K_(n)S_d and S2_(n)S_d
required by the theorem has been supplied.

Suggested insertion before Gate 5: a small lemma with the following table.

    (S2)_1 S3 <= S3, (S2)_2 S3 <= S2, (S2)_3 S3 <= K;
    K_1 W4 <= S3, K_2 W4 <= S2, K_3 W4 <= K;
    L_1 N5 <= W4, L_2 N5 <= S3.

The next modes in the first two lines are scalar; higher ones vanish by
weight. Explain current provenance with the example

    k_1(a_0 b)=(k_0 a)_1 b+(k_1 a)_0 b+a_0(k_1 b).

For a general weight-two a the actual commutators are

    [L_1,a_n]=(2-n)a_(n+1)+(L_1 a)_n,
    [L_2,a_n]=(3-n)a_(n+2)+3(L_1 a)_(n+1)+(L_2 a)_n.

Explain why the primitive multiplets are quasiprimary: their current types
do not occur in the weight-one current space, and L_1 is current-equivariant.
The derivative and affine-normal portions require the general formulas.
In particular, L_2(a_(-1)b) has the scalar term (L_2a)_(-1)b; it cannot
silently be dropped. On the other hand, (L_2a)_1=0 when it occurs in the
displayed positive-mode recursion, since L_2a is scalar.

After Gate 5, display at least the first and last of these consequences,
or all four in a compact aligned display:

    2a_1p = L_1(a_0p)-a_0L_1p in W4,
     a_2p = L_1(a_1p)-a_1L_1p in S3,
    2a_3p = L_2(a_1p)-a_1L_2p in S2,
     a_4p = L_2(a_2p)-a_2L_2p in K,  p in P4.

These are the missing bridge from the two zero-mode gates to the all-mode
hypothesis; they should not be left implicit in a comprehensive textbook.
Sources: hi_cft_c8_proof.tex:835--912 and 1063--1075.

2.3 c02_finite_closure.tex:378--384 -- work one neutral Jacobi reduction.
The paragraph is accurate but too compressed compared with the carefully
explained charged gate. Use the actual schematic decomposition

    e_0 a3 = sum_i t_i (x_i)_0 y_i + n,  n in N3,

where x_i,y_i are charged. Applying zero modes to p3 reduces the first
term to commutators of charged zero modes, already controlled on W4. Then

    e_0((a3)_0 p3)=(a3)_0(e_0 p3)+(e_0 a3)_0 p3 in N5.

The nonzero bottom-triplet coefficient yields the required neutral identity.
This explicitly uses both Gate 4 and the charged part of Gate 5; it is not
just an appeal to current covariance. Source: hi_cft_c8_proof.tex:1024--1057.

3. Parafermion identification: show the dependency order and simplicity
=====================================================================

3.1 c03_factors.tex:214--225 -- acyclicity needs more than the list 6,7,8.
The iterate formula is correct, and the printed a,a; a,b; a,d rows include
the simple poles and the crucial weight-six decoupling. The text should
nevertheless show why evaluating those iterates does not already require
the unknown b,b or d,d products.

A compact dependency table suffices. Let A_r mean evaluating an external
a mode on normal polynomials of weight <=r, and similarly B_r for b.

    A6 -> (b,b) -> A7 -> (b,d) -> B6 -> A8 -> B7 -> (d,d).

After moving Virasoro creation operators outward and removing derivatives,
the possible two-factor non-Virasoro cores are

    <=6: :aa:;
    <=7: :aa:, :ab:;
    <=8: :aa:, :ab:, :bb:, :ad:.

There cannot be three such factors below weight nine. This explains every
arrow and is substantially more useful than an assertion that an order can
be chosen. For the final arrow print

    d_n s=a_1(b_n s)-a_0(b_(n+1)s)
             +b_(n+1)(a_0s)-b_n(a_1s),

whose first two terms use A8 and last two use B7 when s=d.
Source: hi_cft_c8_manuscript_structure.tex:125--188.

3.2 c03_factors.tex:227--235 -- justify the actual factor's simplicity.

The claim that common closure forces only one degree-zero vacuum line is

4. Regularity and vacuum projectivity
====================================

4.1 c04_regularity.tex:73--104 -- cite the regular=>C2 fact, not a new
missing hypothesis.
On first reading the argument appears to demand C2-cofiniteness of the A_i
in addition to the definition. This is not a mathematical gap: regularity
implies C2-cofiniteness, by Li's theorem. A short reminder before the module
quotient theorem will prevent confusion. Primary sources checked directly:
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9807077 (Li, regular=>C2-cofinite);
https://arxiv.org/html/math/0204021 (Abe--Buhl--Dong, Theorem 4.5 and the
module-cofiniteness conclusions).
Thus do not describe this as an extra conjectural property of the components.

4.2 c04:106--135 -- positive assessment.
The argument on V_n intersect C2(V)^perp is correct. High partial-energy
eigenspaces lie inside C2(V); self-adjointness then bounds the expectation
on any vector orthogonal to that subspace. The partial energies need not
commute, and the chapter explicitly avoids simultaneous diagonalization.
The finite number of simple component modules supplies uniform D_i. It is
also right to describe the resulting cutoff as existential rather than a
computed numerical value.

4.3 c04:161--174, 186--201, 250--270 -- positive assessment and one bridge.
The compatible-top lemma is valid: regularity decomposes the entire weak
restriction, and the highest space of a simple ordinary module is its lowest
space. On a generalized weight-zero space, diagonalizability of each partial
energy plus its nonnegative spectrum makes zero trace imply the zero
operator, without needing commuting partial energies or a form on E.
Vacuum-only component tops then force every component translation to vanish.
The vacuum-like lemma correctly converts this into a splitting map.

Add one sentence at c04:250: under C2-cofiniteness the simple composition
factors of the generalized-module category are ordinary modules, so the
preceding ordinary-simple positivity proposition applies to all of them.
This is standard and part of the cited module theory, but its use is not
obvious to a beginning reader. The Abe--Buhl--Dong source above explicitly
states the ordinary-simple conclusion. No unitarity of all U-modules was
assumed in any of these steps, which is an important strength of this proof.

4.4 c04:293--340 -- external rigidity theorem checked; application sound.
I checked Etingof--Penneys 2412.17681v3 directly:
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17681v3, Theorem 1.1.
The exact hypotheses match the chapter: Cauchy-complete linear braided
category, finite-dimensional Hom spaces, End(1)=C, indecomposable X,
1 a summand of Y tensor X, and dim End(X^tensor n)<n! for some n.
The theorem does not require pre-existing tensor exactness.

The finite-algebra realization gives exponential growth using only right
exactness. A nonzero evaluation onto the simple unit splits by its
projectivity. Thus rigidity comes before tensor exactness, and using
Hom(X,-)=Hom(1,- tensor X*) afterwards correctly makes every object
projective. This portion is careful and should be preserved.

Suggested small example: before the application, show why a finite-dimensional
right-exact functor between module categories obeys a bound
dim F(M)<=dim F(P) dim M, by taking a surjection P^(dim M)->M.
It makes the categorical growth estimate concrete without weakening it.

5. Top bound, boundary rank, and arithmetic: add reconstructible examples
========================================================================

5.1 c05:119--127 -- give the four charge multiplicities behind the two
inequalities. This is the highest-priority teaching expansion in c05.
The top table prints dimension, intrinsic weight, and W, but those three
columns do not suffice to reconstruct right-current weight multiplicities.
The minimization formulas do supply them. For the whole space E they give

    mult(H_R=12)=x5,
    mult(H_R=10)=y2,
    mult(H_R=9)=2(x5+x6),
    mult(H_R=7)=2y4.

For a finite sl2 representation, nonnegative weight multiplicities decrease
as the positive weight increases in steps of two. Hence mult10>=mult12 and
mult7>=mult9, exactly y2>=x5 and y4>=x5+x6. The factor two in the odd-charge
rows is the left level-one doublet, not a mysterious multiplicity.

For reproducibility the minimizing absolute j labels for each core top are:
    x0:0; x1:0; x2:0,3; x3:0,2,3,6; x4:6; x5:1,4,6; x6:4;
    y0:0; y1:0,5; y2:0,2,3,5; y3:2; y4:1,2,4,6; y5:1,4; y6:4.
Keep all ties in minimizing m, not merely the smallest absolute charge.
The relevant lattice--Potts ties are, for epsilon=1,
j=1: m=1,-12; j=4: m=4,-9; j=5: m=5,-8;
and for epsilon=tau,
j=3: m=3,-10; j=5: m=5,-8; j=6: m=6,-7.
Negative j gives opposite charges. These ties explain exactly the large
charges entering the inequalities.

The nonnegative certificate itself checks correctly against the table.
Its structure deserves interpretation as a short linear-programming dual
certificate: the two representation constraints absorb the otherwise
negative x5 coefficient, and every remaining coefficient is nonnegative.
This need not introduce any numerical optimization claim.

5.2 c05:61--73 -- finish one minimization example.

5.3 c05:247--257 -- automorphisms and the untwisted pointed subcategory.

5.4 c05:300--336 -- rank-four character obstruction is quoted, not proved
at textbook level yet.
The displayed alternatives exclude six, and the specialization hypotheses
are appropriately stated. But a paragraph about principal-part cancellation
does not let the reader recover any of 15,10,14,42,8,5,12. Since the user
asked for a comprehensive treatment with canonical examples, work one of
these four alternatives in detail: specify the small modular representation,
the relevant theta forms and their first coefficients, the polar unknowns,
and the equation producing the vacuum current number. Then clearly label
the remaining alternatives as the same finite method or as a quoted
specialized calculation. The root's c06 may already cover related character
methods, but it should not leave this exclusion numerically opaque.

This is an exposition gap, not a discovered contradiction in the source.
The m=2 A4 averaging calculation is already a good worked example and its
bound (248-24-32)/12=16 is correct.

5.5 c05:373--389 -- derive at least the dimension identity and one moment.
Six lines of induction data are currently followed immediately by the three
moment equations. A reader should see how the equations are normalized.
The dimension identity is

    9 s_m^2=1+d_m^4+4s_m^2+9d_m^2 sum_j n_j^2,

which reduces to sum n_j^2=4m^2+2. The induction decomposition is

    I(rho)=mX+mY+m(C0+C1+C2)+sum_j n_j Z_j.

Its first and second twist traces are respectively 0 and
3s_m epsilon, where epsilon=nu_2(rho). Substituting the dimensions, and
using d_m^(-1)=d_m-3m, derives the two moment equations. The untwisted
Vec(Z3) input is used to get the three C_j twists, so recall it here.
This gives a natural bridge back to the center/Verlinde foundations.
Source: hi_cft_c8_manuscript_structure.tex:1209--1288.

5.6 c05:439--444 -- six-root uniqueness needs its equality case.
The final conclusion is right, but minimal root count alone leaves the
order-26 quadratic orbit as a competitor. The source excludes it with
the rational coefficient. Briefly show: a Galois orbit supplying the
sqrt(13) term needs six roots; equality permits orders 13 or 26. Their
negative quadratic periods are respectively (-1-sqrt(13))/2 and
(1-sqrt(13))/2. The prescribed rational part picks order 13. Its six
nonresidue roots are Q-linearly independent: a relation would be a degree
at-most-12 polynomial with zero constant term divisible by Phi_13, hence
the zero polynomial. Each root therefore occurs once, forcing n_j=1.
This is an excellent canonical arithmetic example to work completely.
Source: hi_cft_c8_manuscript_structure.tex:1394--1425.
