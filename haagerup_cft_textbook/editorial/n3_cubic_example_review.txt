Conclusion
----------
Yes: every one of the 3^4=81 order-three cubic equations follows from
  delta^2=3delta+1, p+q=1, pq=delta.
The argument below is a complete small-order hand reduction, not merely
a prediction that a checker will return zero. For the textbook, the three
worked representatives in part 2 are the compact teaching insertion; the

This adds no all-order special-function theorem and no positivity claim.

1. Clear denominators before checking the cubic
-----------------------------------------------
Put tau=delta-1 and a=delta-2, so

  B = [[a,-1,-1],[-1,-1,p],[-1,q,-1]],  A=B/tau.

Both delta and tau are nonzero by delta^2-3delta-1=0. The useful reductions
are

  p^2=p-delta,       q^2=q-delta,
  tau^2=delta+2,     a*tau=3,     a^2=5-delta,
  tau^3/delta=7-delta.

For the last identity, tau^3=(delta-1)(delta+2)=4delta-1 and
delta^{-1}=delta-3. Write kappa=7-delta.
The cubic in the chapter, after multiplication by tau^3, is

  C_(g,h)(k,l):=sum_(m=0)^2 B_(m,g+h) B_(g,m+k) B_(h,m+l)
    =tau B_(g+l,k) B_(h+k,l)-kappa 1_(g=0)1_(h=0),       (*).

Every group index is reduced modulo three. The correction is present for
ALL nine choices of k,l when g=h=0. It is not restricted to the all-zero
quadruple. Equivalently, it is a constant all-ones matrix in that block.

2. Three nontrivial representatives, ready for a short insertion
----------------------------------------------------------------
At (g,h,k,l)=(1,1,0,0), the three terms on the left are -1,p,-p^2.
Thus

  C_(1,1)(0,0)=-1+p-p^2=delta-1=tau,

which is the right side, since B_(1,0)^2=1. This illustrates how the
quadratic relation for p, with the PLUS-delta constant term,
p^2-p+delta=0, enters a cubic recoupling identity.

At (1,1,1,1), the left side is p^3-2 and the right side is tau*q^2.
They agree because

  p^3-2=(1-delta)p-delta-2=-tau*p-tau^2,
  tau*q^2=tau(q-delta)=tau(1-p-delta)=-tau*p-tau^2.

The cubic power has introduced no new algebraic parameter.

At (0,0,0,0), the left side is a^3-2. Including the correction, the
residual is

  a^3-2-tau*a^2+kappa
    =-a^2+5-delta=0,

because a-tau=-1 and a^2=5-delta. Omitting the exceptional correction
would leave a nonzero residual. This is a particularly clear small
example of the invertible-channel term in the cubic equation.

An optional fourth check isolates p+q=1 with a mixed input. At
(g,h,k,l)=(0,1,0,2), the left side is

  -a*p-1+q=-a*p-p=-tau*p,

equal to tau B_(2,0) B_(1,2). No correction occurs in this case.

3. A complete four-table hand proof
----------------------------------
Use rows indexed by k=0,1,2 and columns indexed by l=0,1,2. Directly expanding
the three summands gives the following left-side matrices; the displayed
simplifications use only the relations in part 1:

  C_(0,0) =
    [[a^3-2,       -a^2+a-1, -a^2+a-1],
     [-a^2+a-1,    -a^2+a-1,  3a],
     [-a^2+a-1,     3a,      -a^2+a-1]].

  C_(0,1) =
    [[-3,   tau,     -tau*p],
     [tau, -tau*q,   -tau*q],
     [-3,  -tau*p,    tau]].

  C_(1,1) =
    [[tau, tau,       3p],
     [tau, p^3-2,     tau],
     [3p,  tau,       tau]].

  C_(1,2) =
    [[tau,    -tau*q, -3],
     [-3,     -tau*q, tau],
     [-tau*p,  tau,  -tau*p]].

The right-side product matrices B_(g+l,k)B_(h+k,l), before multiplication
by tau, are respectively

  R_(0,0)=[[a^2,1,1],[1,1,delta],[1,delta,1]],
  R_(0,1)=[[-a,1,-p],[1,-q,-q],[-a,-p,1]],
  R_(1,1)=[[1,1,a*p],[1,q^2,1],[a*p,1,1]],
  R_(1,2)=[[1,-q,-a],[-a,-q,1],[-p,1,-p]].

For the (0,0) block, subtraction of kappa in EVERY entry gives the needed
identities

  a^3-2=tau*a^2-kappa,
  -a^2+a-1=tau-kappa,
  3a=tau*delta-kappa.

The first was worked above. The second has both sides 2delta-8, and the
third has both sides 3delta-6. The three nonexceptional blocks agree using
a*tau=3 and p^3-2=tau*q^2. For example, a*delta-2=tau and
-a*p-1+q=-tau*p explain entries in the mixed blocks. Thus (*) is proved
for all k,l at the four stated ordered pairs (g,h).

To cover every other pair, use two exact symmetries:

  (i)  (g,h,k,l) -> (h,g,l,k).
       It swaps commuting scalar factors on each side of (*).

  (ii) (g,h,k,l) -> (-g,-h,-k,-l), together with p <-> q.
       The displayed matrix has B_(-r,-s)(p,q)=B_(r,s)(q,p).
       The three scalar relations are invariant under p <-> q.

The ordered (g,h) pairs split into the four classes
  {(0,0)},
  {(0,1),(1,0),(0,2),(2,0)},
  {(1,1),(2,2)},
  {(1,2),(2,1)}.
The tables cover one representative from each, with all nine k,l choices.
Hence they cover all (1+4+2+2)*9=81 equations, including repeated and
exceptional indices. No generic-index division or discarded zero case is
involved.

4. Relation to the proposed exact checker and reconstruction
------------------------------------------------------------
The natural exact representation is the free rank-four integer normal
form with basis 1,delta,p,delta*p, reduced by
delta^2=3delta+1 and p^2=p-delta; set q=1-p. The cleared residual (*) is
integral in this ring, including its kappa correction. There is no need
for floating roots, radicals, a CAS, or division during the 81-case loop.
Root is drafting that independent checker; none was run for this review.
