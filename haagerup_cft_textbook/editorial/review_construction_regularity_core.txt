Conclusion

The c04 regularity argument is valid with its stated embedded-factor
hypotheses. In particular, the proof does not assume a positive form on
unknown U-modules, simultaneous diagonalization of partial energies, or
tensor exactness before rigidity. The common-core lemma itself is valid.
Its inherited-field application benefits from naming the actual closure;
that clarification is now applied in r04. The reconstructed-sector core
and infinite-multiplicity scope are explicitly covered by the primary
source, not additional conjectures.

1. C2 finiteness: c04:74--90 and 104--151

Small explicitness improvement: write "finitely many" subalgebras in the
definition. The displayed vector sum is conventionally finite, and the
actual family is finite, so this is not an obstruction for U.

The module-cofiniteness premise at 106--116 is correct. A precise source
is Abe--Buhl--Dong, Proposition 5.2: an irreducible weak module over a
C2-cofinite VOA is C2-cofinite. It applies in particular to every ordinary
simple component module. Primary source checked:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0204021
Li's regular-implies-C2 result is also correctly used:
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9807077

If more mechanism is wanted, the relevant finite spanning-set argument
is short: choose finitely many homogeneous C2 representatives. The module
spanning theorem orders their mode indices, makes positive indices
strictly decreasing, and bounds the number of repetitions of nonpositive
indices. In the module C2 quotient, a leftmost mode a_(-r), r>=2, vanishes
because a_(-r)=(T^(r-2)a)_(-2)/(r-1)!. The remaining allowed indices and
their repetitions are bounded, hence only finitely many spanning words
remain. This is a structural spanning theorem, not an explicit computed
cutoff for the Haagerup algebra.

The passage to V, which may have infinitely many copies of component
simples, is also valid. For an algebraic direct sum,
C2,A(direct_sum M_j)=direct_sum C2,A(M_j). Component-module isomorphisms
preserve the intrinsic conformal operator; copies do not acquire arbitrary
intrinsic energy shifts. Finitely many simple types therefore give one
cutoff D_i, even with infinite multiplicity.

For each ambient grade V_n, all eigenvectors of L0^(e_i) with eigenvalue
>D_i lie in C2(V). Thus a vector orthogonal to C2(V) has zero coefficient
on each such eigenvector. This gives the expectation bound separately for
each i, without requiring C2(V) to be invariant under every partial
energy and without requiring the partial energies to commute. The
ambient grading of C2(V), positivity of the inherited form, and finite
dimensionality of V_n are the precise last-step premises. All are stated
or provided by the construction.

2. Component tops: c04:167--218

The lemma is correct. A useful missing teaching sentence is why an
ordinary simple A-module has no additional vectors in Omega_A above its
lowest space: after mode reordering, a vector killed by every lowering
mode generates a module at its own energy and above; if it were above
the lowest energy, this would be a proper nonzero submodule. Thus its
full highest space is exactly its top. The condition must concern modes
of all states, as the chapter already stresses.

The finite invariant E is a module over the semisimple Zhu algebra.
Each partial energy is diagonalizable there with the known simple top
weights. The trace identity establishes reality/nonnegativity of the
ambient lowest weight without choosing any Hermitian form on M. When
the trace is zero, each partial energy is the zero operator, since its
spectrum is nonnegative and it is diagonalizable. The unique-zero-top
condition then places E in actual vacuum summands, where each partial
translation annihilates it. There is no missing positivity assumption.

3. Vacuum splitting: c04:259--296

The finite-length generalized module category is needed before passing
from simple-factor weights to E's generalized weights. This input is
available from C2 cofiniteness and CFT type; it is stated in the proof.
For teaching, explain that generalized eigenspaces are exact in a short
exact sequence of locally finite L0-modules. Induction on a composition
series then gives no negative weights, and exactness at weight zero
shows E_[0] -> V_0 is onto. These are the two uses of the generalized
grading in the vacuum-lifting argument.

The zero modes preserve E_[0], all lowering modes kill it, and it is
finite dimensional. The top lemma applies even though E's ambient L0
could initially have Jordan blocks. Nilpotency makes its trace zero;
the separate nonnegative component spectra force every partial L0 to
vanish. Their vacuum translations vanish too. The vacuum-like lemma
then supplies the section a |-> a_(-1)v, and pi(s(a))=a. Every step
checks; the proof does not invoke semisimplicity of the U-module
category while proving vacuum projectivity.

4. Categorical conclusion: c04:300--403 (line numbers shifted slightly
by foundation's new duality paragraph during this review)

The finite abelian braided category and contragredient duality are
available before rigidity. McRae's review gives the finite category in
Section 2.3, the Grothendieck--Verdier identification in Section 3, and
uses HLZ3 Proposition 4.26 for right exactness:
https://arxiv.org/html/2108.01898
Self-contragredience identifies the dualizing object with the unit.

Foundation's newly inserted Hom(Y tensor X,1)=Hom(Y,DX) paragraph is
correct and supplies the previously compressed explanation of why
evaluation is nonzero. The simple unit makes evaluation an epimorphism;
its projectivity splits that epimorphism. Cauchy completeness and
Krull--Schmidt hold in a finite abelian category; one sentence saying
this would finish the theorem's premise checklist.

The dimension bound uses only preservation of epimorphisms by tensoring.
Iteration is from Z=1 and yields d0(X^n)<=d0(1) C_X^n. The endomorphism
bound is its square. It therefore supplies the factorial inequality
required by Etingof--Penneys Theorem 1.1:
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17681
The theorem does not require exact tensor products. Only after rigidity
does the chapter invoke the two adjoints of tensoring and hence exactness.
Hom(X,-)=Hom(1,- tensor X*) is then exact, so every object is projective.
The order of implications is correct. No change of theorem is needed.

5. Common cores: r04:196--209 and 289--325 before clarification

The common-core lemma needs no core assertion for T*, as its direct-sum
proof shows. For the inherited lattice fields, the chosen smeared
operator is the closure of its finite-energy restriction. CKLW Section
6 defines precisely this closure in equations (106)--(108):
https://arxiv.org/html/1503.01260#S6
For a smooth-energy vector xi, Q_R xi -> xi in every energy Sobolev
norm. An energy estimate ||Y(v,f)eta||<=C||(1+L0)^r eta|| then gives
graph convergence of Q_R xi. This identifies the finite-energy and
smooth-domain closures. The estimate alone would not establish that
domain as a core for an arbitrarily chosen closed extension. The concise
r04 clarification now identifies this actual construction.

For reconstruction, Henriques--Tener Definition 9.4 defines the closed
field from its algebraic domain, Theorem 9.5 identifies it with the
represented net field, and Remark 9.8 extends the results to infinite
multiplicity and nondiscrete spectrum:
https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20735v3#S9
Thus bounded-energy M is the relevant core before ordinaryness. The
book's projection argument uses finite multiplicity only in the chosen
ordinary summand S, not in the whole net sector. The r04 clarification
now names those source components, making the scope auditable. No new
analytic assumption was added.
