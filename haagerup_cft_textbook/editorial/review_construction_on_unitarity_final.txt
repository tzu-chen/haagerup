1. Restore the rationality-to-regularity bridge
----------------------------------------------
r04:114--119 and253 use regularity of U, whereas c04's final theorem
concludes strong rationality. r02:520--528 carefully distinguishes
rationality from regularity but does not state the implication used here.

Add one sentence, preferably at the end of c04's theorem:

  For a VOA of CFT type, rationality together with C2-cofiniteness
  implies regularity; hence every weak U-module is a direct sum of
  ordinary simple modules.

This is the Abe--Buhl--Dong implication, with all its hypotheses already
proved for U. It is needed for the bounded-energy weak module in r04,
not merely for semisimplicity of ordinary modules.
Primary statement checked:
[Abe--Buhl--Dong](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0204021).

2. Make the common-core premise explicit in both applications
------------------------------------------------------------
r04:162--173 and240--270.
The abstract closure lemma is correct. Its applications need one
explicit statement that the domains being used really are cores:

* For the lattice branching, finite-energy vectors form a core for the
  inherited smeared fields, by the energy bounds and spectral cutoffs.
* For reverse reconstruction, the reconstructed smeared operator is
  defined as the closure of its restriction to M. Thus M is its core.

The primary reconstruction theorem agrees with the chapter's use:
Definition9.4 gives this closure convention; Remark9.8 allows the
bounded-energy domain without finite multiplicities or discrete spectrum.
Theorem9.5 identifies the closed represented fields. The Virasoro
identification comes from localized stress smearing and a partition of
unity, as in Corollary9.6.
[Henriques--Tener, Section9](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20735v3#S9).

At269, retain the good wording "as a Hilbert-space equality".
A smeared field need not map M back into M. Mode commutation and
boundedness of P justify the convergent sum; only then does the core
lemma justify closure and adjoint commutation.

For a beginner, define "core" in one sentence before the lemma:
D is a core for T when D is dense in D(T) for the graph norm
(||xi||^2+||T xi||^2)^(1/2). This also explains the approximating
sequence used in145--149. No extra theorem is required.

3. Actually verify the hypothesis of the annulus-domain lemma
------------------------------------------------------------
r04:326--354.
The conditional lemma is correct, and its proof already proves the
stronger assertion M^fin subset Ran A. The chapter then applies it to
arbitrary annulus pairs without yet explaining why AC=lambda r^L0
exists for the chosen geometric system or why A,C are injective with
dense range. The expanded source provides precisely this verification.

Minimal repair:
State that the chosen incoming annulus has inner Jordan domain
containing0. Put a small round disc rD inside that domain and take C
to be the intervening annulus, with matching boundary parametrizations.
Sewing gives the round annulus; choosing its central lift gives
AC=lambda r^L0. The geometric annulus injectivity/dense-range theorem
supplies the remaining assumptions. The outgoing adjoint uses the same
construction.

Also put BOTH conclusions in the lemma statement:
M^fin subset Ran A and A^-1 M^fin=C M^fin is dense.
The range assertion is an actual requirement of an incoming generalized
annulus, not only an intermediate proof line. The inverse is used only
on its range; the existing oscillator example already explains why
there is no everywhere-defined bounded inverse.

4. Specify holomorphy and the insertion domain before continuing
---------------------------------------------------------------
r04:342--371 and403--407.
The finite-Laurent argument and the component-coverage argument are
correct. The chapter should explicitly name the other function to
which the identity theorem is being applied:

  The geometric point-insertion theorem supplies a bounded operator
  G_v(z), weakly holomorphic on Omega_ins subset C^times.

The Laurent matrix coefficient is then holomorphic on the same
punctured domain. Without these words, only one side's analyticity has
been explained. The statements about all components and uniqueness on

for D_A or D_(B*) is needed.

It would also help to define the prescribed insertion interior as
the union over geometric representatives of the same operator pair.
The existing instruction to repeat the argument then visibly covers
all its points. Empty-interior pairs are already handled correctly
by the BA identity.

5. Give "bounded localized vertex operators" its actual role
------------------------------------------------------------
r04:114--119,295--309,423--428.
The final positivity result does not follow from strong unitarity
and the displayed pair identity alone for an arbitrary VOA.
Its premises include a simple regular unitary VOA with bounded
localized vertex operators, and the corresponding representation
condition for every ordinary module.

This property is available here by inheritance from the lattice
theory, but it is not explicitly defined or named in the textbook's
first step. Boundedness for each fixed state v is weaker than the
standard bounded-insertion condition: for a fixed allowed insertion,
one permits an additional s^L0 damping in the state variable so the
bilinear map on two Hilbert spaces is bounded. The generated local
algebras must then commute at disjoint intervals.

A concise definition before the bounded-insertion subnet discussion,
and a sentence at423 listing simple + regular + unitary + bounded
localized vertex operators, would close this presentation gap.
The annulus identity then supplies exactly the module-representation
condition, and finite sums cover all ordinary modules by regularity.
This agrees with
[Tener, Definition2.18 and Corollary4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.08257v3).
It does not require importing an unproved charged-intertwiner locality
theorem or assuming a tensorator for the preceding linear functor.

6. Correct the scalar-deck statement for reducible modules
---------------------------------------------------------
r04:376--381.
Replace "a module of lowest weight h" by "a simple ordinary module
of lowest weight h" (or "a module with spectrum in h+Z").
For a general ordinary direct sum with several integer-shift classes,
having a lowest weight h does not imply exp(2pi i L0)=exp(2pi i h).
For example U plus a1/3-weight module has two deck phases.
The paired cancellation remains correct for arbitrary finite sums,
because every U-mode still commutes with the deck operator.
This is a one-word correction, consistent with r02's improved
ordinary-module definition.

7. One useful concrete formula for the paired lift
--------------------------------------------------
r04:384--395.
The cancellation is right, but "localizing both with one common
diffeomorphism" is the chapter's most compressed technical notion.
The following single display would make the operation reviewable:

 A_M=U_M(delta_tilde)^* pi_M(U(delta) A_U),
 B_M=pi_M(B_U U(delta)^*) U_M(delta_tilde).

Explain that the common localizer delta is chosen so that the
operators inside pi_M belong to the interval algebra, and the same
lift delta_tilde is used twice. This makes the opposite deck factors
and the locality of BA visible. The geometric transport theorem
justifies this display. The actual source also matches the incoming
semigroup/framing normalization; if omitted here, name that matching
as part of the comparison theorem being invoked.

This is a pedagogical recommendation, not a demand for the full
semigroup proof. The current text's pairwise scope is already correct.

"separating": x Omega=0 for x in N_H(I) implies x=0.
The reason is locality and cyclicity of Omega for the complementary
interval: x kills every y Omega with y in N_H(I'), a dense set.
This makes the local-irreducibility proof accessible without an
operator-algebra prerequisite chapter.

  Complementary projections give an actual direct-sum decomposition

  inclusion's index argument. Complete rationality is first transferred
  only as an isomorphism-invariant property.

  It does not assume the full stabilizer Aut(B/U) is trivial.

  through an unavailable tensor equivalence.
* The projection onto one ordinary summand maps every bounded-energy
  space into finitely many of that summand's finite grades. This
  removes, rather than assumes away, infinite sector multiplicities.

  of an algebraic module isomorphism is correct.

  star-comparison. The chapter explicitly postpones fusion positivity.
