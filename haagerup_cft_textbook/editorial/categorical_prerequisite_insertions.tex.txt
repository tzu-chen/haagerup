In formulas, choose a left dual $X^*$ with maps
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_X:X^*\otimes X\to\one,
 \qquad \operatorname{coev}_X:\one\to X\otimes X^*.
\]
With associators understood, the two straightening identities are
\[
 (1_X\otimes\operatorname{ev}_X)
 (\operatorname{coev}_X\otimes1_X)=1_X,
 \qquad
 (\operatorname{ev}_X\otimes1_{X^*})
 (1_{X^*}\otimes\operatorname{coev}_X)=1_{X^*}.
\]
For finite-dimensional vector spaces they say
$v=\sum_i e_i e^i(v)$, using a basis and its dual.
In a fusion category the same equations concern junction maps;
they do not require the line $X$ to be an ordinary vector space.

A pivotal structure is a natural monoidal identification
$j_X:X\to X^{**}$. It lets us close an endomorphism $f:X\to X$
without changing the orientation of its line. One closure gives
\[
 \Tr_{\rm cat}(f)
 =\operatorname{ev}_{X^*}
   \bigl((j_Xf)\otimes1_{X^*}\bigr)
   \operatorname{coev}_X.
\]
Sphericity says that closing on the other side gives the same scalar.
The quantum dimension is $d_X=\Tr_{\rm cat}(1_X)$.
For $X=\bigoplus_a a^{\oplus m_a}$, an endomorphism consists of
matrices $f_a$ on the multiplicity spaces, and
\[
 \Tr_{\rm cat}(f)=\sum_a d_a\Tr(f_a).
\]
Thus on $\one\oplus\tau^{\oplus2}$ in the Fibonacci category,
an endomorphism $(\lambda,B)$ with $B\in M_2(\C)$ has trace
$\lambda+\varphi\Tr(B)$. Its identity has trace $1+2\varphi$,
not the unweighted sum of multiplicities $3$. This weighting is the
same one that will appear in modular link traces and twist moments.

\[
 \Hom(Y\otimes X,\one)\simeq\Hom(Y,DX).

At $Y=DX$, the identity of $DX$ corresponds to the evaluation

\subsection{Why a Lagrangian algebra recovers a center}
\label{sec:lagrangian-reconstruction}
The tensor unit of the module category $\mathcal M_A$ is $A$ itself.
Its dimension there must be one even when its dimension in
$\mathcal M$ is large. The normalization is
\[
 \FPdim_{\mathcal M_A}(M)
   =\frac{\FPdim_{\mathcal M}(M)}{\FPdim_{\mathcal M}(A)},
\]
where the numerator forgets the module action. In our positive
spherical setting these are also the categorical dimensions.
For instance, in $Z(\operatorname{Vec}_{\Z_3})$ take
$A=\bigoplus_k(0,k)$. The flux module
$x_g=\bigoplus_k(g,k)$ has ambient dimension three and module
dimension one. The three flux modules consequently have global
dimension three, as the dimension formula predicts. Their actions
and relative tensor products are worked out in
Chapter~\ref{ch:defects}.

There is also an actual functor behind reconstruction. The free
module functor is
$G_A:\mathcal M\to\mathcal M_A$, $G_A(X)=X\otimes A$.
It is \emph{central}: the ambient braiding supplies coherent
half-braidings of $G_A(X)$ with every $A$-module. Equivalently,
it lifts to a braided tensor functor
\[
 \widetilde G_A:\mathcal M\longrightarrow Z(\mathcal M_A).
\]
This is the meaning of a central functor; it need not take values in
a braided category before the lift.

\begin{theorem}[Lagrangian reconstruction]
\label{thm:lagrangian-reconstruction}
Let $\mathcal M$ be a nondegenerate braided fusion category and
$A$ a connected commutative separable algebra. If
$\FPdim(A)^2=\FPdim(\mathcal M)$, then the central lift gives
a braided equivalence
$\mathcal M\simeq Z(\mathcal M_A)$, carrying $A$ to the canonical
algebra $I(\one)$.
\end{theorem}
The structural input is that this lift is fully faithful. Once that
is known, the dimension formulas explain why the Lagrangian condition
makes it exhaustive:
\[
 \FPdim Z(\mathcal M_A)
 =\left(\frac{\FPdim\mathcal M}{\FPdim A}\right)^2
 =\FPdim\mathcal M.
\]
A fully faithful fusion subcategory with the full positive global
dimension cannot omit any simple object. This general theorem is
what turns a holomorphic extension $H/U$ into the identification
$\Rep(U)\simeq Z(\Rep(U)_H)$ later in the construction.

For fusion categories the analogue of a representation is a
\emph{module category}. In a right-action convention it consists
of a finite semisimple category $\mathcal N$, an action
$(m,X)\mapsto m\triangleleft X$, and coherent isomorphisms
\[
 (m\triangleleft X)\triangleleft Y
   \simeq m\triangleleft(X\otimes Y),\qquad
 m\triangleleft\one\simeq m.
\]
Their coherence is the module version of the pentagon. A module
endofunctor $T$ also has coherent isomorphisms
$T(m\triangleleft X)\simeq T(m)\triangleleft X$:
it respects the action, rather than merely mapping objects to objects.
Such functors form the \emph{dual category}, with composition as
tensor product. If $\mathcal N$ is indecomposable as a module
category, this dual is a fusion category. Choosing a left-action
convention requires the corresponding reversal of composition order.

For a small example, let untwisted $\operatorname{Vec}_{\Z_3}$
act on $\operatorname{Vec}$ by forgetting the grading. Each simple
group label acts as the identity functor. A linear endofunctor is
$T_W(v)=W\otimes v$; respecting the generator's action equips
$W$ with an operator $U$ satisfying $U^3=1$. The three one-dimensional
eigenvalue choices $1,\zeta_3,\zeta_3^2$, where
$\zeta_3=e^{2\pi i/3}$, are the three simple module
endofunctors, and composition multiplies their characters. Thus the
module category has one simple object, whereas its dual has three.
Fusion categories related through such an indecomposable module
category are Morita equivalent. Their simple objects and fusion
rings can differ; their centers agree by the theorem below.
