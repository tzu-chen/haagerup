\section{Fusion paths and operators}

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:chain-dimension}}
The recurrence gives
\[
 t_2=11,\ t_3=36,\ t_4=119,\ t_5=393,\ t_6=1298,
 \ t_7=4287,\ t_8=14159.
\]
For odd length the extra term in \eqref{eq:path-dimension} vanishes,
so $\dim\HH_{\mathrm{path},5}=393$. For even length it is four, giving
$\dim\HH_{\mathrm{path},8}=14163$. The eigenvalue $d>1$ strictly dominates the
other five eigenvalues in modulus. The alphabet bound $6^L$ counts
all words, most of which violate at least one fusion constraint.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:chain-projector}}
Let $v_f$ be the conjugated $f$th column in the convention of
\eqref{eq:rho-projector}. Unitarity says $v_f^\dagger v_g=\delta_{fg}$.
For $P_S=\sum_{f\in S}v_fv_f^\dagger$, multiplication gives
$P_S^2=P_S=P_S^\dagger$, and its range is the $|S|$-dimensional
span of those columns. With fusion multiplicities, a channel has
an additional orthonormal multiplicity label, and one sums over
all selected copies. It is the subspace, not a single scalar label,
that defines the projection.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:chain-orbit}}
For a basis vector $|x\rangle$ with stabilizer $K\subset G$, form
\[
 v_\chi=\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\chi(g)}\,|gx\rangle.
\]
Every repeated orbit entry is multiplied by
$\sum_{k\in K}\overline{\chi(k)}$. Character orthogonality makes
this zero unless $\chi|_K=1$. In the surviving case, choose one
representative per coset and divide its character sum by
$\sqrt{|G|/|K|}$. Distinct cosets give orthogonal basis vectors,
so this is normalized. It has the specified character eigenvalue
under the group action.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:chain-noninvertible}}
Using self-adjointness and $Y_\rho^2=1+3Y_\rho$,
\[
 (Y_\rho-2)^2=Y_\rho^2-4Y_\rho+4=5-Y_\rho.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $d-2$ and $-d^{-1}-2$, both nonzero. On a unit
vector,
$5-d\leq\|(Y_\rho-2)s\|^2\leq5+d^{-1}$.
An orthogonal projection has only eigenvalues zero and one;
these values plainly do not have that property. The special
extraction formula in the rank-four vacuum module uses one
particular input vector, not a global projection identity.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:chain-boundary}}
For $L$ physical rho objects between fixed external heights $a,b$,
the dimension is $(N_\rho^L)_{ab}$: matrix multiplication sums
all intermediate fusion histories. Summing the diagonal entries
instead identifies the two ends and permits every compatible
starting height. It changes both the state space and the closing
Hamiltonian terms. Even when the infinite bulk theory agrees,
fixed endpoints can select boundary conformal families or carry
edge states absent from a given periodic sector. The equal leading
growth $d^L$ does not equate their finite spectra.

\section{Finite-size scaling}

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:scaling-residual}}
Expand $u=\sum_nc_n|n\rangle$. Then
$\|r\|^2=\sum_n|c_n|^2(E_n-E)^2$. This is at least the squared
distance from $E$ to the spectrum, since the weights sum to one.
If $\Pi$ projects onto the target cluster and all other eigenvalues
are at least $\gamma$ from $E$, the same sum is at least
$\gamma^2\|(1-\Pi)u\|^2$. Hence the unwanted squared weight is
at most $\|r\|^2/\gamma^2$. The value of $\gamma$ is a separate
spectral input.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:scaling-stress}}
For the vacuum, $L_n|0\rangle=0$ for $n\geq-1$ and
$L_0|0\rangle=0$. Therefore
\[
 \|L_{-n}|0\rangle\|^2
 =\langle0|[L_n,L_{-n}]|0\rangle
 =\frac c{12}n(n^2-1).
\]
At $n=2$ this is $c/2$. The source amplitude squared is
$(2\pi v/L)^2c/2$, whereas the stress gap squared is
$(4\pi v/L)^2$. Their ratio is $c/8$, proving the estimator.
The calculation assumes the source has the displayed Virasoro
normalization; it does not establish the lattice-to-CFT map.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:scaling-improvement}}
Put $k=2\pi m/L$ and use periodic indices. In the first sum
below, relabel $i=j+1$:
\[
 \sum_j e^{-ikj}(B_{j+1}-B_j)
 =e^{ik}\sum_i e^{-iki}B_i-\sum_j e^{-ikj}B_j.
\]
The phase is periodic because $e^{ikL}=1$, so the difference
is $(e^{ik}-1)B_m$. It vanishes identically for $m=0$, as
required for the unchanged total Hamiltonian.

For fixed nonzero $m$, matrix elements of a scaling field
$B_j$ of dimension $\Delta_B$ between normalized low-energy
states scale as $L^{-\Delta_B}$. Summing its Fourier component
over $L$ sites gives $O(L^{1-\Delta_B})$ when the relevant
continuum matrix element is nonzero. Since
$e^{ik}-1=2\pi i m/L+O(L^{-2})$, the improvement contributes
$O(L^{-\Delta_B})$. A dimension-one current can therefore
contribute at the same $L^{-1}$ order as the extensive stress
Fourier mode. A field of dimension greater than one is
subleading under this scaling assumption. Selection rules may
make a particular matrix element vanish; dimension counting
alone does not guarantee a nonzero coefficient.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:scaling-casimir}}
Write
$E_0(L)/L=e_\infty-A/L^2+r(L)$, with $A=\pi vc/6$.
Subtracting the two sizes eliminates $e_\infty$ and gives
\begin{align*}
 (vc)_{\mathrm{two\ sizes}}
 &=-\frac6\pi
 \frac{E_0(L_1)/L_1-E_0(L_2)/L_2}
 {L_1^{-2}-L_2^{-2}},\\
 &=vc-\frac6\pi\frac{r(L_1)-r(L_2)}
 {L_1^{-2}-L_2^{-2}}.
\end{align*}
Thus even small per-site remainders can matter when divided by
the difference of two small inverse-square lengths. Two exact
energies solve for two leading coefficients exactly, but do not
estimate this remainder.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:scaling-fraction}}
If $\phi$ is scalar of dimension $\Delta_\phi$, then
$L_{-1}\phi$ has weights
$(\Delta_\phi/2+1,\Delta_\phi/2)$ and spin one. A holomorphic
current has weights $(1,0)$. The former has nonzero right weight
whenever $\Delta_\phi>0$. A momentum-one level near
$\Delta_\phi+1$ is therefore consistent with an ordinary
descendant and is not evidence for a current. It could also mix
with an unrelated finite-size primary.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:scaling-modular}}
Let $D=\beta\partial_\beta$ and $a=\pi c/(6\beta_*)$. Then
\[
 \frac{D Z}{Z}=\beta(a-\mu).
\]
Its vanishing at $\beta=\beta_*$ gives $a=\mu$. At that point,
$Z^{(j)}/Z=(-1)^jm_j$ for $j\geq2$, where the derivatives
are in $\beta$ and the moments are centered. Repeated use of
$D\beta^j=\beta^j(D+j)$ gives
\begin{align*}
 D^3&=\beta\partial_\beta+3\beta^2\partial_\beta^2+
       \beta^3\partial_\beta^3,\\
 D^5&=\beta\partial_\beta+15\beta^2\partial_\beta^2+
       25\beta^3\partial_\beta^3\\
 &\hspace{2em}+10\beta^4\partial_\beta^4+
       \beta^5\partial_\beta^5.
\end{align*}
The first-derivative term vanishes. Substitution gives exactly
the third and fifth conditions in the chapter. The odd
derivatives vanish because modular $S$ acts by $t\mapsto-t$
in $\beta=\beta_*e^t$. A charge projection inserts a symmetry
operator into the torus trace. Exchanging cycles generally
turns that insertion into a spatial twist; the projected
partition function alone is therefore not $S$-invariant.

\section{Fields, descendants, and overlaps}

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-phases}}
The phase of $\lambda^3$ is
$3\theta_\phi+3\theta_\chi-3\theta_\psi$. Multiplication by
$c_\psi$ and division by $c_\phi c_\chi$ cancel it. The same
exponent bookkeeping proves the other two invariants. Multiplying
their definitions gives $J^3=I_\nu I_\kappa/I_\lambda$.
If a symmetry maps each field to a unit phase times its adjoint,
it conjugates each three-point coefficient. All unit phases
cancel in $I_\lambda$, so invariance says
$I_\lambda=\overline{I_\lambda}$. No claim that individual
coefficients are real was required.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-descendant}}
The commutator identity
\[
 L_1L_{-1}^n|h\rangle
 =n(2h+n-1)L_{-1}^{n-1}|h\rangle
\]
follows by commuting $L_1$ past the $n$ lowering operators.
Taking the norm recursively proves $n!(2h)_n$. The first three
terms of the scalar vacuum-source series are
\[
 1+\Delta^2+
 \left[\frac{\Delta(\Delta+1)}2\right]^2+\cdots.
\]
The equal-time integral near coincidence contains
$\int_0^\varepsilon\theta^{-2\Delta}\,d\theta$, finite
precisely for $2\Delta<1$. At the endpoint it diverges
logarithmically.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-cumulant}}
Treat the normalized overlaps as a linear moment functional
with $\langle1\rangle=1$, and set $\widetilde X_i=X_i-m_i$.
Then
\[
 B_{ij}=\langle\widetilde X_i\widetilde X_j\rangle,
 \qquad K=\langle\widetilde X_1\widetilde X_2\widetilde X_3\rangle.
\]
After $X_i\mapsto u_iX_i+v_i$, the new centered variable is
$u_i\widetilde X_i$. Multilinearity proves the factors
$u_iu_j$ and $u_1u_2u_3$, including for complex data. The
third leg's $u_3$ denotes its amplitude factor after converting
the outgoing ket change into a bra change.

A physical branch insertion does not invalidate these affine
identities. It changes the moment functional itself, replacing
three-point moments by normalized four-point correlators.
Their connected part contains extra OPE data. Thus it invalidates
the inference that the invariant equals the original three-field
coefficient, unless an additional Ward or selection argument
removes that branch contribution.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-filter}}
Each retained-state summand is nonnegative. For every omitted
state, its exponential factor is at most
$e^{-2\tau(E_{\rm cut}-E_\phi)}$. Factoring this out of the
omitted sum proves the bound. To see why residuals do not provide
the cutoff, take a diagonal Hamiltonian with energies $0,1,2$
and retain the exact eigenvectors at $0$ and $2$. Both residuals
vanish, but the omitted state has energy $1<2$. A source with
weight on all three detects the failure of using the largest
retained energy as an omitted-state threshold.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-response}}
Differentiate $g_a/g_b$ and insert the Hellmann--Feynman
derivatives. Under $V\mapsto V+a1+bH$, the gap derivatives
become $d_a+bg_a$, so the added numerator is
$bg_ag_b-bg_ag_b=0$. A coupling reparametrization multiplies
every coordinate derivative by the same Jacobian. Comparing
its direction with size drift assumes the perturbation probes
the same running continuum coupling and that other corrections
are negligible. The algebraic cancellation of energy rescaling
does not prove either premise.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-current}}
Write $u'_i=\sum_k u_kU_{ki}$ and
$v'_j=\sum_l v_lV_{lj}$. The gluing amplitude is linear in
both incoming kets, giving $M'=U^TMV$. Both $U^T$ and $V$
are unitary, so $M'^\dagger M'$ is unitarily conjugate to
$M^\dagger M$ and has the same eigenvalues.

For normalized pairs, the tensor-product difference satisfies
\[
 \|u\otimes v-u'\otimes v'\|
 \leq\|(u-u')\otimes v\|+
      \|u'\otimes(v-v')\|\leq\delta_1+\delta_2.
\]
Taking an inner product with a unit parent cannot increase that
norm. Divide by $|A_{000}|$, subtract the continuum gluing error
$\eta$, and use $|x^TMy|\leq\|M\|_2$ for normalized coefficient
vectors. This proves the stated bound. A small denominator
amplifies an absolute vector error; cancellation of a parent's
overall normalization is not cancellation of its directional
state error.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:overlap-branch}}
Expanding either side gives
$2b^4-4b^3+6b^2-4b+2$. Consequently
\[
 \sum_{i<j}\frac{\delta B_{ij}}{B_{ij}}
 =\frac{2N_\Lambda}{c}
   \frac{1+(b-1)^4+b^4}{b^4(b-1)^4}
 =2\frac{\delta K}{K}.
\]
This is exactly the cancellation in the logarithmic variation
of the estimator. Multiplying the identity in $q,r$ by
$b^4(b-1)^4$ reduces it to the same polynomial identity.
The quadratic variation is a bilinear Hessian $H(v_b,v_b)$;
differentiation controls $H(v_b,\partial_bv_b)$. It supplies
no identity for $H(v_b,v_{b'})$ at independent $b,b'$, so two
separately regulated singularities require another calculation.

\section{Tensor networks}

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:MPS-schmidt}}
The largest two Schmidt probabilities sum to $5/6$. Their
normalized truncation is
\[
 |\Psi_2\rangle=\sqrt{3/5}|00\rangle+\sqrt{2/5}|11\rangle.
\]
Its squared overlap with $\Psi$ is $5/6$, its phase-aligned
vector-norm error is $\sqrt{2-2\sqrt{5/6}}$, and its energy
is $-5/6$. The best rank-one energy is $-1/2$.
The proof by the projector onto the left Schmidt support
already optimizes over every subspace of the prescribed
dimension. It therefore includes arbitrary local basis
changes; it is not restricted to coordinate truncation in
the original product basis.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:MPS-edge}}
The only nonzero block of $A^a$ in block row $a$ and column $b$
is $W_{ab}$. In $A^{x_0}A^{x_1}$, the first tensor's column
must therefore be the second tensor's row $x_1$, leaving
$W_{x_0x_1}$ times the next block row. Induction produces
$W_{x_0x_1}\cdots W_{x_{L-2}x_{L-1}}$ followed by the last
row. The ordinary trace forces its last column back to $x_0$,
giving $W_{x_{L-1}x_0}$ and the desired cyclic fiber trace.
Any forbidden edge makes one factor zero. This proof works
for unequal fiber dimensions, because consecutive rectangular
maps have matching intermediate spaces.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:MPS-correlation}}
The norm matrix is $(t^2-1)I+J$. Its eigenvalues are $t^2+2$
on the uniform vector and $t^2-1$ twice on the color-orthogonal
plane. The nontrivial length is
\[
 \xi=-\left[\log\left|\frac{t^2-1}{t^2+2}\right|\right]^{-1}.
\]
At $t=0$ the ratio is $-1/2$, giving alternating color
correlations and finite length $1/\log2$. At $t=1$ it is zero;
the uniform edge amplitudes give an ordinary product state on
the supported three colors. At large $|t|$, the ratio approaches
one and the length grows, reflecting long domains of equal
colors. No conformal limit follows merely from this parameter
limit.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:MPS-cat}}
Across any nontrivial cut the three distinct product branches
are orthogonal on both sides. Their Schmidt probabilities are
$(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, so the entropy is $\log3$. It is independent
of interval length. The state has three ordered product components
and a nondecaying order correlator; its standard symmetry-breaking
parent has a finite ground-state degeneracy. Neither
feature gives the logarithmic interval growth associated with
a CFT central charge.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:MPS-resolvent}}
Normalize $(l|r)=1$ and put $\mathcal P=|r)(l|$. The fixed-point
relations imply $\mathcal E\mathcal P=\mathcal P\mathcal E=
\mathcal P$. Thus
$\mathcal E^n=\mathcal P+(\mathcal E-\mathcal P)^n$ for
$n\geq1$. Subtracting the product of one-point functions in
a separated correlator removes the $\mathcal P$ term.
On the complementary space the spectral radius is less than
one for an injective clustering tensor, so the geometric
series converges even if the map is not diagonalizable:
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}(\mathcal E-\mathcal P)^n
 =(1-\mathcal E+\mathcal P)^{-1}.
\]
The additional stationary direction of a cat remains after
subtracting only one rank-one projector. A periodic peripheral
root likewise has modulus one. The series then fails to
converge on its coupled direction, and a connected primitive
component must be specified before using this formula.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:MPS-scaling}}
At $n=1,2,\infty$, the logarithmic coefficients in
\eqref{eq:FES} are respectively $c/6,c/8,c/12$.
Let $x_a=\log\xi_a$ and $x_b=\log\xi_b$. For
$S_1=ax+b+u/x$, the secant is
\[
 \frac{S_1(x_b)-S_1(x_a)}{x_b-x_a}
 =a-\frac{u}{x_ax_b}.
\]
The corresponding central-charge diagnostic is six times this
quantity. Increasing $\xi$ by a large factor may still change
the logarithms modestly, so the correction can drift slowly.
This illustrative correction is not a derived law for the
Haagerup sequence; it shows why apparent slope stability needs
an independently justified remainder model.

\section{Gapped vacua and parent Hamiltonians}

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:gapped-PF}}
On the neutral basis $(f,s)$, with
$s=(o_0+o_1+o_2)/\sqrt3$, the rho matrix is
$R_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\sqrt3\\\sqrt3&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Its characteristic polynomial is $x^2-3x-1$, giving $d$ and
$-d^{-1}$. On the color-orthogonal plane, $J$ vanishes and
rho is $-P$, where $P$ is the reflection of two colors. Hence
rho squares to one on that plane. Alpha is the order-three
rotation and $P\alpha P=\alpha^{-1}$.

On the neutral plane, $1+\alpha+\alpha^2=3$ and
$R_0^2=1+3R_0$. On the charged plane that alpha sum is zero
and $(-P)^2=1$. These two checks prove the full fusion relation
$R^2=1+(1+\alpha+\alpha^2)R$, as well as
$R\alpha=\alpha^{-1}R$. They identify the representation as
the two scalar fusion representations plus the two-dimensional
charged representation.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:gapped-cubics}}
The ordered total probability is $p=3/(b^2+3)$ and the fixed
probability is $1-p$. Thus
\[
 \langle\Phi^3\rangle=p\,p^{-3/2}=p^{-1/2},\qquad
 \langle\Phi^\dagger\Phi E\rangle=p\,p^{-1}b/\sqrt3=b/\sqrt3.
\]
For the neutral cubic,
\[
 \langle E^3\rangle
 =\frac{b^3/\sqrt3-3\sqrt3/b}{b^2+3}
 =\frac{b^2-3}{\sqrt3 b}=-\frac1{\sqrt3},
\]
using $b^2+b=3$. Checking the multiplication identities at
the ordered and fixed points separately proves them as
identities of functions, not only as identities of averaged
moments. Substituting $g=b/\sqrt3$ yields
$C_{EEE}=(g^2-1)/g$ and $p^{-1}=1+g^2$.

\subsection*{Exercises~\ref{ex:gapped-parent} and \ref{ex:gapped-short}}
Invariance of a few global vectors constrains the operator only
on their span. It says nothing about its action on the local
range with arbitrary boundary data and arbitrary outside
environment. The stronger statement is that the open line
maps every interval module vector, with all allowed endpoint
and MPO boundary indices, into the complete local module
range. Closure under adjoints makes this range reducing, so
its orthogonal projector commutes with the closed line.

For the short Hamiltonian, one still needs equality of its
global zero space with the proposed MPS family and a positive
gap above that zero space uniformly in ring size. A different
longer parent can penalize directions left unpenalized by the
short terms. Its gap does not provide an operator comparison
bounding the short Hamiltonian from below by a positive
multiple of that parent. The four-site kernel count supplies
a concrete obstruction to the simplest such comparison.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:gapped-filter}}
With $B=Y_\rho-2$ and $B^\dagger B=5-Y_\rho$, commutation
with $H$ gives
\[
 E_B=\frac{5E-\langle HY_\rho\rangle}
                  {5-\langle Y_\rho\rangle}.
\]
Subtracting $E$ gives the covariance formula. The denominator
is at least $5-d>0$, and
$\operatorname{Var}(Y_\rho)\leq(d+d^{-1})^2/4=13/4$.
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bounds the total energy difference
by a constant times $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(H_L)}$.
For variance $O(L)$, division by $L$ gives $O(L^{-1/2})$.
A cat with nonzero weights on branches of densities $e_1\ne e_2$
has a contribution $w_1w_2(e_1-e_2)^2L^2$ to the variance.

\section{Candidate tests}

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-product}}
Independent stress tensors make the dimension of a product
field the sum of its factor dimensions. The mixed three-point
function factors into two normalized two-point functions,
giving $\lambda=1$ in the displayed product convention.
Moreover $c_\psi=c_\phi c_\chi$, hence $I_\lambda=1$.
Under independent rephasings, $\lambda$ itself can acquire a
phase, while $|\lambda|$, all dimensions, and $I_\lambda$
remain unchanged. Thus the invariant comparison is stronger
than comparing a printed phase of one matrix element.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-Narain}}
On a totally isotropic subspace, projection to the positive
two-dimensional factor has zero kernel: a vector with positive
part zero must also have negative part zero by nullness. The
projection is injective, so the subspace has dimension at most
two. For light scalar charges the integral pairing has absolute
value strictly below one and must vanish. At a non-strict
endpoint the value $\pm1$ is not excluded, so the planar
reduction requires a separate argument.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-triad}}
The only nonzero charge-neutral ordered triples are the six
permutations of $(p_1,p_2,p_3)$. Each coefficient product is
$1/(\sqrt3)^3$, giving $2/\sqrt3$. All three external dimensions
are equal, exactly the case excluded by the unequal-shell
hypothesis. Arbitrary degeneracy within unequal shells was
already allowed by that theorem; it is equality of the three
radii that changes the geometry.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-crossing}}
Write $q(z)=(1-z)^{2\delta}$ and $P(z)=q(z)g_D(z)$.
Then $F_D(z)=P(z)-P(1-z)$, so the first and third derivatives
at $1/2$ are respectively $2P'$ and $2P'''$. At that point,
\[
 \frac{q'}q=-4\delta,\qquad
 \frac{q''}q=8\delta(2\delta-1),\qquad
 \frac{q'''}q=-32\delta(2\delta-1)(\delta-1).
\]
Insert $g_D''=2g_D'+8Cg_D$ and
$g_D'''=8(C+1)g_D'$, where $C=D(D-1)$, into the product
rule. With the chapter's $A=1-6\delta+6\delta^2$ and
$B=1-3\delta+2\delta^2$, this gives
\[
 \frac{P'''}q=8(C+A)g_D'-32\delta(3C+B)g_D,
 \qquad \frac{P'}q=g_D'-4\delta g_D.
\]
Substitution into the defined functional yields
\[
 \frac{\mathcal L(F_D)}{g_D}
 =(C+A-\kappa)r_D-4\delta(3C+B-\kappa),
 \qquad r_D=g_D'/g_D.
\]
For the identity $D=0$, use $g_0=1$ directly rather than the
positive-dimension integral representation.

The arithmetic in the certificate is exact:
\[
 \frac54\frac9{10}(.083)=.093375,\qquad
 \frac{169}{100}\frac{37}{40}(.246)=.3845595,
\]
and hence $.303+.093375-.3845595=.0118155>0$.
The identity, charged, and epsilon contributions have a positive
total under the stated lower bounds. Every additional exchange
outside $(.16,1.75)$ has nonnegative coefficient and positive
functional value, so crossing requires another coupled exchange
inside that interval.

Application to the chain requires a unitary local continuum
CFT with the specified normalized Hermitian scalar and a positive,
convergent block expansion. The proposed dimensions and OPE
magnitudes must be limiting continuum values, or controlled
intervals over which the strict inequality survives. Finite
eigenvector overlaps alone do not establish those premises.
The additional interpretation as a new two-dimensional scalar
uses integer locality, even spin in the identical-scalar OPE,
and the descendant analysis in the chapter. It does not require
assuming a particular central charge or a named Haagerup CFT.
It also does not exclude the continuum when the required extra
scalar is present.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-orbifold}}
The oscillator formula gives
$h_L=11/288$ and $h_R=35/288$. Their sum is $23/144$,
but that pair is not scalar: its spin is $-1/12$.
Nonnegative excitations and zero modes imply that a scalar
must have both weights at least $35/288$, hence
$\Delta\geq35/144$. Level matching or projection can
increase this threshold; the bound need not be attained.

If $a^3=1$ in the abelianization and $\chi^2=1$, then
$\chi(a)^3=\chi(a)^2=1$, so $\chi(a)=1$. This applies
to each determinant character independently. For the fixed
Hermitian axis, the unit normalization
$X=(e^{i\theta}\phi+e^{-i\theta}\phi^\dagger)/\sqrt2$
gives $|C_{XXX}|=|c_\phi|/\sqrt2$ at a cubic extremum.
The isometric lift contributes no group-order factor.
Thus $|c_\phi|/\sqrt2\leq\sqrt{2/3}$ gives the stated
$2/\sqrt3$, with no further rescaling.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-Potts}}
The triples $w_i^3$ contribute $c_\sigma^2a_i^3$, since two Potts
factors contribute their order cubics and the third contributes
the identity. The six permutations of $w_1w_2w_3$ pair each
Potts order field with its adjoint and contribute
$6a_1a_2a_3$. Every other triple violates an independent
ultraviolet charge. In $C(\sigma_1,\chi,\sigma_2^\dagger)$
only $w_1$ survives, giving $\lambda=a_1$ and hence
$|\lambda|\leq1$. A large cubic can arise from several
charge-conserving permutations without increasing this one
mixed matrix element.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-norm}}
The conjugate of $\varphi$ is $(1-\sqrt5)/2$. Therefore
\[
 N(\varphi+2)=\frac{(5+\sqrt5)(5-\sqrt5)}4=5,
 \qquad
 N\!\left(\frac{64(\varphi+2)^3}{3}\right)
 =\frac{64^2 5^3}{9}.
\]
An algebraic integer has an integer field norm, so the second
number proves that the proposed quotient is not an algebraic
integer. Fusion-subcategory divisibility then excludes an
order-three pointed subcategory from the ambient category of
defects preserving the original retained algebra, and hence
from its smaller maximal-chiral-trivial symmetry kernel.
It does not control a symmetry that permutes stress tensors
and acts nontrivially on the chiral algebra. The separate
three-cycle argument, together with the maximal graph pairing,
is what bounds those chiral images and prevents independent
left-right cycles.

\subsection*{Exercise~\ref{ex:identification-residues}}
Reduce the sums modulo one, keep values strictly between zero
and $1/2$, and then double them. Adding zero to the Fibonacci
residues retains only $1/5$, yielding $\Delta=2/5$. Adding
$1/2$ retains $1/10$ and $3/10$, yielding $1/5$ and $3/5$.

For a scalar below dimension one, positive-energy integer
grading leaves no freedom to add a nonzero integer to its
chiral weight while remaining below $1/2$. Therefore twist
residues impose this necessary condition. The full pairing
can remove possible entries, and the category does not fix
their actual multiplicities or lowest characters. An
intermediate smaller algebra has a different representation
category, so this residue list cannot be imposed on it by
performing a further mathematical condensation absent from
the physical theory.
