\section{Solutions on conformal fields and vertex algebras}

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:cft-level1}.}
The commutator $[L_1,L_{-1}]=2L_0$ gives, by induction,
\[
 L_1L_{-1}^n|h\rangle
 =n(2h+n-1)L_{-1}^{n-1}|h\rangle.
\]
Taking an inner product and using $L_{-1}^\dagger=L_1$
therefore multiplies the previous norm by $n(2h+n-1)$.
Starting with a unit primary gives $n!(2h)_n$.
For equal external weights, each three-point descendant coefficient
is $(p)_n$ for an exchanged primary of weight $p$.
The block is consequently
\[
 z^p\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(p)_n^2}{n!(2p)_n}z^n
 =z^p\left(1+\frac p2z+
       \frac{p(p+1)^2}{4(2p+1)}z^2+\cdots\right).
\]
The identity block is treated separately at $p=0$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:cft-null}.}
Let $v=(L_{-2}+aL_{-1}^2)|h\rangle$. Acting with $L_1$
gives $[3+a(4h+2)]L_{-1}|h\rangle$, so at $h=1/16$
we need $a=-4/3$. The $L_2$ action is
$4h+c/2+6ah$. At $c=1/2,h=1/16$ it equals
$1/4+1/4-1/2=0$. Thus
\[
 \left(L_{-2}-\frac43L_{-1}^2\right)|1/16\rangle
\]
is the null vector. Higher positive modes annihilate it by
grading. Its vanishing is the input for the Ising differential
equation in the sewing chapter.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:cft-compact}.}
For $(m,n)=(1,1)$, $\bar h=0$ requires $R^{-1}-R/2=0$,
hence $R=\sqrt2$. Then $p_L=\sqrt2$ and $h=p_L^2/2=1$.
Under $(R,m,n)\mapsto(2/R,n,m)$, $p_L$ is unchanged
and $p_R$ changes sign. Both conformal weights are unchanged.
The holomorphic current at the fixed radius is the exponential
current of the $A_1$ lattice example.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:cft-stress-combination}.}
The fourth-order pole of $(T_1+T_2)(z)
(c_2T_1-c_1T_2)(0)$ is
$(c_2c_1-c_1c_2)/2=0$, and the remaining singular poles
are $2A/z^2+\partial A/z$. Thus $A$ is primary for the
total stress. Orthogonality of the two stress states gives
\[
 \norm A^2=\frac12c_1c_2(c_1+c_2)>0.
\]
Its self-OPE has second-order coefficient
$2(c_2^2T_1+c_1^2T_2)$, which is generally not $2A$.
A primary of weight two is therefore not automatically a
Virasoro stress.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:cft-thermal}.}
Put $t=v\beta/L$. The vacuum contribution to $Z(it)$ at large
$t$ is $e^{\pi ct/6}$, because the cylinder vacuum energy
uses $c_L+c_R=2c$. Modular invariance replaces $t$ by $1/t$.
As $\beta\to0$,
\[
 \log Z\sim\frac{\pi c}{6t}=\frac{\pi cL}{6v\beta}.
\]
If the vacuum is not normalizable, the low-temperature trace is
controlled by the actual spectral threshold and its measure.
One cannot insert a unit-degeneracy vacuum term; this is where
effective central charge and possible power or logarithmic
prefactors enter.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:cft-marginality}.}
One needs normalized OPE coefficients controlling quadratic beta
functions; higher-order integrated correlators and contact terms
controlling further obstructions; and an analysis of redundant
directions, current recombinations, and symmetry constraints.
Vanishing beta functions must hold on a locus of couplings after
these identifications. A count of thirty-six dimension-two
primaries alone supplies none of those conclusions.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:voa-weight}.}
The singular products have $n=0,\ldots,6$ and respective weights
$6,5,4,3,2,1,0$. Products with $n>6$ vanish because their
weights would be negative. CFT type gives $V_0=\C\one$,
so $a_{(6)}b$ is a scalar multiple of the vacuum; $a_{(4)}b$
is the weight-two candidate. This list permits a product to be
zero; weight counting does not establish its nonvanishing.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:voa-cocycle}.}
Write the exponent of the sign as a bilinear form $B(a,b)$
over $\Z/2\Z$. Bilinearity gives
\[
 B(a,b)+B(a+b,c)=B(b,c)+B(a,b+c),
\]
which is exactly the two-cocycle identity. For the triangular
root-basis choice, $B(a,b)-B(b,a)$ is
$\sum_{i>j}G_{ij}(a_ib_j+a_jb_i)$ modulo two.
The diagonal terms in $(a,b)$ are even, so this is $(a,b)$
modulo two. Hence the commutator ratio is $(-1)^{(a,b)}$.
A coboundary has the form
$f(a)f(b)/f(a+b)$ and is symmetric in $a,b$; it cancels
out of that ratio.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:voa-rankone}.}
The oscillator series begins $1+q+2q^2+\cdots$.
For the vacuum lattice, $\Theta_L=1+2q+O(q^4)$, giving
\[
 \Theta_L\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-1}=1+3q+4q^2+\cdots.
\]
For the shifted lattice, the momentum weights are
$(m+1/2)^2$. Thus
\[
 \Theta_{L+\alpha/2}=q^{1/4}(2+2q^2+\cdots),\qquad
 \frac{\Theta_{L+\alpha/2}}{\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)}
 =q^{1/4}(2+2q+6q^2+\cdots).
\]
The two shortest momenta explain the two-dimensional top.
The module is nevertheless invertible: adding its discriminant
coset twice gives $L$. An invertible simple has categorical
dimension one, independently of its top multiplicity.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:voa-heisenberg-c2}.}
Every oscillator $J_{-n}\one$ with $n\ge2$ is a derivative,
and derivatives vanish in the $C_2$ quotient. Commuting
oscillators shows that only powers of $J_{-1}\one$ remain.
The Heisenberg Fock basis has no relation among these powers;
equivalently the associated polynomial symbols of the
$C_2$ terms contain an oscillator of index at least two.
The quotient is $\C[x]$, an infinite-dimensional algebra.
The VOA is strongly generated by $J$, so this is the desired
counterexample to an implication from finite strong generation.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:voa-coset}.}
Subtract the charge-one Cartan boson:
\[
 \frac{3k}{k+2}-1=\frac{2(k-1)}{k+2}.
\]
At $k=3$ and $13$ this is $4/5$ and $8/5$.
Identification with the parafermion factors additionally needs
their generating fields, OPEs, null relations, and the proof
that the relevant simple quotient is realized. Central charge
is only one invariant of a VOA.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:voa-gauge}.}
The transported basis norms are $4,40,40$, since $g^{-1}$
rescales each vector by the inverse of the stated factor.
Each of the last two coefficients has squared modulus
$(13+27)/400=1/10$. Therefore
\[
 \norm v_g^2=4+40/10+40/10=12.
\]
Adding coefficient magnitudes alone would give $6/5$,
which uses the old orthonormal form on the new coordinates.

\section{Solutions on characters and unitarity}

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:char-oscillator}.}
The five partitions give, respectively,
\[
 8,\quad8^2,\quad\binom92,\quad
 8\binom92,\quad\binom{11}{4}.
\]
Their sum is $8+64+36+288+330=726$.
The $E_8$ theta coefficients through weight four are
$1,240,2160,6720,17520$. Hence the VOA coefficient is
\[
 17520+6720(8)+2160(44)+240(192)+726=213126.
\]
This separates momentum degeneracy from oscillator degeneracy.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:char-primary}.}
Through grade three, multiplication by
$P(q)=1-q-q^2+\cdots$ gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 &v(0)&v(1)&v(2)&v(3)\\\hline
 b&80&1170&9300&53162\\
 d_4&5&224&2504&16975
\end{array}
\]
For example, $v_b(3)=65042-10630-1250=53162$.
Convolution with $1/P(q)=1+q+2q^2+3q^3+\cdots$
returns
$a(1)=v(1)+v(0)$,
$a(2)=v(2)+v(1)+2v(0)$, and
$a(3)=v(3)+v(2)+2v(1)+3v(0)$.
These recover all the displayed character entries.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:char-pole}.}
A twist determines $h$ modulo one, so the three stated
weights have the same twist. The possible $2/13$ top would
produce a pole with coefficient $\gamma$. Existence of the
specified fundamental modular solutions, together with
$\dim U_1=6+13\gamma$ and the independent six-current theorem,
forces $\gamma=0$. The uniquely selected fundamental solution
then has its first nonzero $d_3$ coefficient at $15/13$.
Excluding $2/13$ by itself would not exclude $28/13$;
that final selection uses the known fundamental solution.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:char-equal}.}
The Grothendieck group is free on the isomorphism classes
of simples. Taking a character is a linear map from that group
to a space of functions, and the map need not be injective.
Defect insertions, including tube projectors, can distinguish
equal unflavoured characters. Flavoured traces or matrix
elements of specified current zero modes can also distinguish
modules when the associated actions differ.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:char-uniqueness-scope}.}
If $v$ is a modular-invariant constant vector, adding $tv$
does not change any negative-power coefficient. The compact
modular-curve argument then proves uniqueness only modulo
such invariant constants. One must prescribe the projection
of the constant coefficient onto the invariant subspace,
or an equivalent value normalization, to remove the ambiguity.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:unitarity-damping}.}
Set $a=-2\log r>0$. The continuous function
$f(x)=(x+1)e^{-ax}$ has derivative
$e^{-ax}[1-a(x+1)]$. Thus
\[
 \sup_{n\ge0}(n+1)r^{2n}\le
 \begin{cases}
 1,&a\ge1,\\
 e^{a-1}/a,&0<a<1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Choosing an integer nearest $1/a-1$ also shows that the
discrete supremum diverges like $1/(ea)$ as $a\downarrow0$.
The estimate is finite for every proper annulus and loses
uniformity as the damping annulus degenerates.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:unitarity-projector}.}
Because $P$ preserves the common core, $1-P$ preserves it too,
and $T_0(1-P)=(1-P)T_0$. Apply the same graph-limit
argument to both projections. Their ranges then give a
direct-sum decomposition of the \emph{closed operator and its
domain}. The adjoint of this direct sum is the direct sum of
the adjoints, proving reduction of $T^*$. Only proving
invariance of one subspace would not by itself give this
direct-sum statement.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:unitarity-counting}.}
Full faithfulness identifies each image endomorphism algebra
with $\C$, so the image of a simple is simple in the target
semisimple category. It also preserves the vanishing of
Hom spaces between distinct simples, making the twelve
images pairwise nonisomorphic. They exhaust the twelve target
simples, and additivity exhausts finite direct sums.
A tensorator would be a coherent natural isomorphism
$F(X)\otimes F(Y)\to F(X\otimes Y)$.
Counting objects and morphism dimensions neither supplies
these maps nor verifies their pentagon and braiding compatibilities.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:unitarity-deck}.}
A full rotation on the $h=1/3$ module is
$e^{2\pi i/3}$, while on the integer-graded vacuum VOA it is
one. If $v\in U$ is homogeneous, every mode changes energy
by the integer $\wt(v)-n-1$. Hence every mode commutes
with $Z=e^{2\pi iL_0}$, and
$BZ^mY(v,z)Z^{-m}A=BY(v,z)A$ on the test domain.
Bounded extension preserves this equality. The paired
cancellation does not make the individual charged-sector
phase equal to one.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:unitarity-end}.}
For four mutually inequivalent summands, Schur's lemma gives
$\C^4$, of dimension four. If $Y\cong Y^*$, the $Y$
multiplicity space is two-dimensional, giving
$\C\oplus\C\oplus M_2(\C)$, of dimension six and
noncommutative. The canonical extension argument translates
these dimensions into the rank-four and rank-six alternatives.
It is the repeated simple, rather than four displayed summands,
that produces a matrix block.

\section{Solutions on full spectra and correlation functions}

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:full-relevant}.}
Squaring the seven top dimensions gives
\[
 3^2+5^2+7^2+13^2+14^2+27^2+42^2
 =9+25+49+169+196+729+1764=2941.
\]
At dimension $23/13$, the $d_4$ top pairs with a grade-one
primary. The multiplicity for each spin $+1$ or $-1$ is
$5(229-5)=1120$. Similarly the $d_1$ contribution at
$25/13$ is $7(292-7)=1995$ for each sign.
Counting both signs doubles these numbers.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:full-E8states}.}
At chiral weights $(1,1)$ there are $248^2=61504$
scalar primaries. At each of $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$,
the chiral weight-two space splits into $3875$ primaries,
$248$ current derivatives, and one stress state.
Thus the total is
\[
 61504+2(3875)+2(248)+2=69752.
\]
The last $498$ states are not Virasoro primaries.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:full-tail}.}
For each omitted state, factor
$E^ke^{-bE}=e^{-b_0E}[E^ke^{-(b-b_0)E}]$
and bound the bracket by its supremum for $E\ge E_D$.
Positivity permits summation. With $a=b-b_0>0$, the
suprema are
\[
 \sup_{E\ge E_D}Ee^{-aE}=
 \begin{cases}E_De^{-aE_D},&aE_D\ge1,\\
 (ae)^{-1},&aE_D<1,\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{E\ge E_D}E^2e^{-aE}=
 \begin{cases}E_D^2e^{-aE_D},&aE_D\ge2,\\
 4/(a^2e^2),&aE_D<2.\end{cases}
\]
Heat capacity uses the variance
$\langle E^2\rangle-\langle E\rangle^2$, so one needs
control of the zeroth, first, and second weighted sums,
including their common denominator. A small unweighted
tail can still carry a substantial energy moment.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:full-onevac}.}
There are six dimension-zero states, which describes a
multiple-vacuum or disconnected situation instead of the
specified connected theory with a unique vacuum.
Division by six can destroy integral multiplicities and
does not construct identity fields, OPEs, or sewing maps.
A numerical rescaling of a partition function cannot repair
missing local-theory data.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:full-symmetry}.}
The $36=6^2$ count is the diagonal theory's scalar
dimension-two subspace invariant under all its Verlinde lines.
The $1488=6\cdot248$ count is invariant under the selected
first-factor $\mathcal H_3$ action in the extended full theory.
The $61504=248^2$ count is the entire scalar dimension-two
space of that extended theory, with no symmetry restriction.
Different ambient Hilbert spaces and line actions are being used.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:full-boost}.}
The two block products are
\[
 X^{\mathsf t}\eta=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-\varepsilon\\
 \varepsilon^{\mathsf t}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \eta X=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\varepsilon\\
 -\varepsilon^{\mathsf t}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They sum to zero. Thus $e^{tX}$ preserves the quadratic
form $p^{\mathsf t}\eta p$, and its infinitesimal action is
the stated charge boost. For $r=1$ and $\varepsilon=-1$,
$X^2=I$ and $e^{tX}=I\cosh t+X\sinh t$.
Starting at $(p_L,p_R)=(\sqrt2,0)$ yields
$(\sqrt2\cosh t,-\sqrt2\sinh t)$.
The spin remains $(p_L^2-p_R^2)/2=1$, although
$\bar h=\sinh^2t>0$ for $t\ne0$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:corr-fifteen}.}
Write the momenta at $(\infty,1,x,0)$ as
$(-a_i,a_j,-a_k,a_\ell)$. Neutrality requires the
multisets $\{i,k\}$ and $\{j,\ell\}$ to agree.
There are three choices with all labels equal. For $i\ne k$,
the choices $(j,\ell)=(i,k)$ and $(k,i)$ give six each.
The finite-distance factors are
$x^{-(a_k,a_\ell)}(1-x)^{-(a_j,a_k)}$.
They are, respectively,
$x^{-4}(1-x)^{-4}$,
$x^{-4}(1-x)^{-2}$, and
$x^{-2}(1-x)^{-4}$.
Each assignment has coefficient $1/9$, giving the three
terms in the chapter.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:corr-blocks}.}
At order four the blocks of exchanged weights one, two,
and three contribute $2/3$, $21/5$, and $29/3$.
Subtracting them from the reduced-correlator coefficient gives
\[
 A_4=\frac{65}{3}-\frac23-\frac{21}{5}-\frac{29}{3}
     =\frac{107}{15}.
\]
In the charged ordering the reduced correlator is
$x^4G_+(x)=(4/3)x^6+\cdots$, so the first exchanged
global primary has weight six and squared coefficient $4/3$.
The corresponding unit-normalized three-point coefficient
has magnitude $2/\sqrt3$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:corr-stress}.}
Only pure-factor contractions survive, and
$\langle T_i(\infty)T_i(1)T_i(0)\rangle=c_i$.
For $A=c_2T_1-c_1T_2$ the unnormalized three-point
coefficient is $c_1c_2(c_2^2-c_1^2)$.
Divide its square by
$\norm A^6=[c_1c_2(c_1+c_2)/2]^3$ to obtain
\[
 C_{\widehat A\widehat A\widehat A}^2
 =\frac{8(c_2-c_1)^2}{c_1c_2(c_1+c_2)}.
\]
When $c_1=c_2$, exchanging the two factors sends $A$ to
$-A$, so its odd correlator vanishes. Its positive norm
does not vanish; it remains a nonzero field.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:corr-spin3}.}
At the Potts point $5c_0+22=26$ and
$\lambda(5c_0+22)=(4/13)26=8$, so the weight-four
primary norm is zero. For the simple pole in the four-point
function, $\frac12\partial b$ contributes
$\frac12(4B)=2B$ and
$-\frac1{12}\partial^3T_0$ contributes
$-72N/12=-6N=-2c_0$.
The total is $2B-2c_0$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:corr-lightgram}.}
Using $q=(2,0,-2)$ gives
\[
 K=\frac1{39}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 24&-13&-11\\
 -13&26&-13\\
 -11&-13&24
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The vectors $(1,1,1)$, $(1,0,-1)$, and $(1,-2,1)$
are eigenvectors with eigenvalues $0$, $35/39$, and $1$.
They are mutually orthogonal and span the space, so $K$
is positive semidefinite. Positivity concerns the quadratic
form, not the signs of its individual matrix entries.

\section{Solutions on defects and sewing}

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:defect-projector}.}
Modular orthogonality makes the eigenvalue of $\Pi_{\rm inv}$
equal to $\delta_{i0}$ in sector $i$. Therefore it squares
to itself and is Hermitian on the orthogonal sector
decomposition. Its vacuum eigenvalue before normalization
is $\sum_a d_a^2=\mathcal D^2$, which explains the
denominator. Dividing by $\mathcal D$ would leave the
vacuum eigenvalue $\mathcal D$ and would fail idempotence.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:defect-twined}.}
On the scalar blocks, $\alpha=1$ and the two possible
$\rho$ eigenvalues solve $x^2=1+3x$.
On the two-dimensional block,
$\alpha^3=1$, $\rho^2=1$, and conjugation by the swap
matrix sends $\alpha$ to $\alpha^{-1}$. Moreover
$1+\alpha+\alpha^2=0$, so
$\rho+\alpha\rho+\alpha^2\rho=0$ there. These checks
verify the generating relations and hence all products.
The traces of $\alpha$ on the three multiplicity blocks
are $1,1,-1$; those of $\rho$ are $d,-d^{-1},0$.
Multiplying by the respective characters and the unchanged
antiholomorphic factor gives the two twined traces.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:defect-alpha}.}
The forgetful table gives
$R\alpha=c_0\oplus c_1\oplus c_2$.
The smallest weight is $1/3$ and its top multiplicity is
three. In the first-factor twisted Hilbert space this pairs
with the antiholomorphic $E_8$ vacuum, giving fractional
spin. A twisted state sits at a defect endpoint or in a
defect boundary condition; the ordinary untwisted locality
requirement of integer spin does not apply to it unchanged.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:defect-between}.}
For $i\ne j$,
$(\alpha^i\rho)^*(\alpha^j\rho)
=\alpha^{i-j}+\rho+\alpha\rho+\alpha^2\rho$.
The nontrivial invertible term restricts to
$c_0+c_1+c_2$, contributing three states of weight $1/3$.
Each noninvertible contributes another three. The total is
$3+3\cdot3=12$, compared with nine in the self-boundary
case, where the invertible term is the vacuum boundary.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:defect-categories}.}
Six counts the simples of the Haagerup boundary fusion
category; twelve counts those of its braided Drinfeld center;
thirty-six counts the simples of the relevant bimodule defect
category equivalent, up to the stated opposite convention,
to a product of the boundary category with its opposite.
The center is braided; the boundary and bimodule categories
are used as fusion categories. A Lagrangian algebra has
only the trivial local module category, although its all-module
category has six simples. Replacing all modules by local
ones would discard precisely the nonlocal boundary sectors.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:sewing-Ising}.}
For $0<z<1$, put $u=\sqrt{1+\sqrt{1-z}}$ and
$v=\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-z}}$. Then
$u^2+v^2=2$ and $uv=\sqrt z$.
Consequently
$(u\pm v)/\sqrt2=\sqrt{1\pm\sqrt z}$.
The common factor $[z(1-z)]^{-1/8}/\sqrt2$
is unchanged by $z\mapsto1-z$, proving the Hadamard
fusing relation. It mixes both blocks: the continued
identity block is $(\mathcal F_1+\mathcal F_\epsilon)/\sqrt2$.
Its absolute square therefore cannot equal
$|\mathcal F_1|^2$ for all $z$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:sewing-pairing}.}
Write $D=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\\bar b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The relative phase of the two monodromy eigenvalues is $-1$.
With the convention $G=\mathcal F^{\mathsf t}D\overline{\mathcal F}$,
the condition $M^{\mathsf t}D\overline M=D$ forces $b=0$.
For $D=\operatorname{diag}(a,d)$, the condition
$F^{\mathsf t}D\overline F=D$ with the real Hadamard $F$ forces $a=d$.
Thus $D=aI$. Positivity gives $a\ge0$, and a nontrivial
theory with unit vacuum coefficient has $a=1$.
Because $\mathcal F_\epsilon\sim\frac12z^{3/8}$,
the energy-channel bulk coefficient is $1/4$, giving
$|C_{\sigma\sigma\epsilon}|=1/2$.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:sewing-object}.}
An equivalence of full local theories preserves all
correlators and their sewing structures. An algebra-object
description specifies the chiral multiplication, unit, and
compatibilities that enter the rational full-theory construction.
Forgetting multiplication leaves an underlying module, which
still remembers how the chiral algebra acts. Taking its
character retains only energy multiplicities. Thus OPE
products are lost on passing to the module, and most
representation matrices are lost on passing to the character.
An equality at a weaker level does not reconstruct the stronger
one. In the rational construction, Morita equivalence of
appropriate algebra objects can describe equivalent full
theories even when the algebra objects are not isomorphic.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:sewing-coset}.}
If infinitely many orthogonal states have energy $E\le E_*$,
each contributes at least $e^{-\beta E_*}>0$ to the trace.
The partial sums are unbounded for every $\beta>0$.
A common vacuum-energy shift only multiplies this divergence
by a finite positive constant. Positivity rules out cancellations;
an alternating formal series is not a positive thermal trace.

\paragraph{Exercise~\ref{ex:sewing-descent}.}
One needs the chosen reduced Hilbert space and its left-right
pairing, bounded or otherwise controlled line operators on
that space, junction maps with the specified associator,
compatibility with the local OPE, and sewing consistency.
For a nontrivial branching extension, one also needs coherence
with the complementary-sector multiplicities and the algebra
object defining the reduction. A torus trace can detect
spectra and certain line eigenvalue sums. It does not record
the junction associator or all the local and higher-surface
sewing identities.
