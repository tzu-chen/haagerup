HIGH-VALUE SMALL REPAIRS

1. f01_fusion.tex:76--78: semisimplicity and finiteness are conflated.

   Current: 'Semisimplicity permits a finite list of simple line types.'
   Semisimplicity supplies decomposition; finiteness of the simple list is
   a separate assumption in the displayed definition.

   Replace with:
   'Semisimplicity means that every object decomposes into simples; the
   separate finiteness assumption limits the list of simple line types.'

   At 124--125, optional short gloss:
   'Natural and monoidal mean compatible with all morphisms and tensor
   products.' This is enough to decode the pivotal definition without
   introducing a separate discussion of natural transformations.

2. f02_haagerup.tex:257: K_0 appears without definition. It also returns in
   the character exercise about equal traces versus distinct modules.

   Suggested replacement opening:
   'The Grothendieck ring K_0(H3) is the free abelian group on the six
   simple-object classes, with addition induced by direct sum and
   multiplication induced by fusion. Allowing complex coefficients gives
   the six-dimensional algebra A=K_0(H3) tensor_Z C.'

   Nearby two tiny terminology fixes:
   - 247--249: the undefined symbol mathsf A_{0,0} is unnecessary here.
     Say the repeated entry is 'the same displayed value
     [F^{rho rho rho}_rho]_{rho rho} within each category'.
     The A-coordinate notation can wait until f05 where it is defined.
   - 311--312: write the adjoint relation explicitly as Y_x^dagger=Y_{x*}.
     This makes the later self-adjointness of Y_rho and orthogonality of
     P_+ transparent. At the earlier first mention around108, say
     'the closed-line operator Y_rho'.

3. f03_centers.tex:17--39: give the promised concrete operator-algebra and
   graph vocabulary before using it. The later r04 definition currently
   arrives several chapters after first use.

   STATUS: ROOT-INTEGRATED during this review. Root reports the von Neumann,
   outer-action, principal-graph, and L^2 trace-completion sentences are now
   in f03. The wording below records the reviewed proposal, not pending work.

   Proposed opening replacement:
   'A von Neumann algebra is a unital adjoint-closed algebra of bounded
   operators on a Hilbert space, closed in the weak operator topology
   (convergence of all matrix elements). It is a factor when its algebraic
   center consists only of scalar multiples of the identity.'

   After the fixed-point example:
   'Outer means that no nonidentity group element acts as x -> u x u*
   for a unitary u inside M.'

   Near the basic-bimodule/even-parts paragraph:
   'Write X={}_N L^2(M)_M for the basic bimodule. The principal graph is
   rooted and bipartite: its even vertices are the simple N--N bimodules
   and its odd vertices the simple N--M bimodules generated by alternating
   X with its dual. An even vertex Z is joined to an odd vertex Y with
   multiplicity dim Hom(Y,Z tensor_N X), and the root is L^2(N); reversing
   N and M gives the dual principal graph.'

   This provides exactly the graph definition later used in f06. No graph
   classification or extra example is required.

   Near the II_1 calculation at 53--58, a useful single sentence is:
   'Here L^2(M) is the Hilbert completion of M for
   ||x||_2^2=tr(x* x), with commuting left and right multiplication.'
   This is stated in the finite-trace setting, not as a finite-trace
   construction for arbitrary type-III factors.

4. f03_centers.tex:103: the two-strand Jones--Wenzl projection is named
   before any definition, although this case needs only the displayed
   Jones projection.

   Replace the noun phrase with:
   'the two-strand Jones--Wenzl projection 1-e_1
   =1-delta_TL^{-1} U_1'.
   It is visibly the complementary projection, and its dimension is the
   difference already computed in the example.

5. f03_centers.tex:204--209: 'dagger Frobenius algebra' is a central
   technical ingredient but the Frobenius compatibility is never written
   before it is used. The later sewing chapter describes its role verbally.

   After defining Delta=mu^dagger, add, with associators suppressed,

     (mu tensor 1)(1 tensor Delta)
       = Delta mu
       = (1 tensor mu)(Delta tensor 1).

   Suggested introduction:
   'The Frobenius condition says that Delta=mu^dagger respects both
   A actions; suppressing associators, ...'

   Together with the existing unit, associativity, adjoint and
   mu mu^dagger=beta 1 normalization, this tells the reader what the term
   means. It also explains directly why beta^{-1}Delta is the separable
   splitting; this last observation is optional.

6. f03_centers.tex:443--450: the nine scalar half-braidings are correctly
   listed, but their completeness has a one-sentence hidden step. The
   preceding section specifically warns that a simple central object can
   forget to a nonsimple object, so a reader may reasonably ask why that
   does not produce extra simples in this example.

   Add after the character calculation:
   'On an arbitrary graded object, the half-braiding with the generator
   acts on each graded multiplicity space by an operator of order three.
   It diagonalizes into the three characters, so every center object
   decomposes into these pairs (g,k).'

   This is elementary linear algebra, and it closes the classification
   without appealing to an unintroduced quantum double.

7. r02_vertex_algebras.tex:697--701,754--759: two technical terms remain
   less concrete than surrounding definitions.

   PCT:
   Replace 'a positive Hermitian form on its physical real form' by
   'a canonical positive Hermitian form and an antilinear PCT involution'.
   The Hermitian form is on the complex VOA; the present 'on its physical
   real form' wording can distract. Add:
   'PCT denotes the conjugation entering the radial-adjoint identity;
   it preserves the vacuum and conformal vector.'
   There is no need to identify it with coordinatewise conjugation, and
   the existing next sentence correctly says why one must not do that.

   Admissible:
   'An admissible module is a weak module with a compatible nonnegative
   integer grading M=sum_{n>=0} M(n), such that a_(m) shifts the grade
   by wt(a)-m-1. This grading need not itself be the L_0 eigenspace
   grading, and finite-dimensional pieces are not part of the definition.'
   Then rationality can be stated as complete reducibility of admissible
   modules, without the vague word 'appropriate'. The formula only uses
   the already-established mode weight rule. The strong-rationality
   definition is supplied in c04, so a forward reference there is enough.

8. r03_characters.tex:116--123: the compact modular-curve proof is correct,
   but 'cusps', 'kernel subgroup', and compactification arrive together.
   A short explanation of the geometric object makes the uniqueness
   theorem teachable with standard complex analysis.

   Suggested replacement middle of proof:
   'Let Gamma be the kernel of the finite-image modular representation;
   it has finite index in SL_2(Z). The quotient Gamma\H becomes a compact
   Riemann surface after adding its finitely many cusps, the orbits of
   rational boundary points and infinity. Absence of negative powers in
   the local q-expansions means that each component extends holomorphically
   over those added points. A holomorphic function on a compact Riemann
   surface is constant by the maximum principle.'

   The previous modular covariance sentence explains why regularity at
   infinity gives regularity at every cusp. Retain that premise rather
   than implying a single arbitrary q-expansion controls all cusps.

   132--134: explain 'Xi_{:,j}' with 'the colon denotes the full
   twelve-component column'. This is array notation, not standard
   self-explanatory mathematical punctuation.
