Reviewer: root. Source snapshot: 2026-10-05 approximately 18:07 UTC.

This review does not rebuild Lean or execute gamma numerical integrals.

Checks:
1. The regularized gamma integral has the correct sign for the stated
   shifts/reflection, and the t->0 Taylor subtraction leaves a bounded
   integrand. The infinity bound is uniform on compact subsets of
   0<Re z<omega1+omega2. Positivity and midpoint value follow directly.
2. The logarithmic shift reduces to the stated sine integral. The residue
   R=sqrt(omega1 omega2)/(2pi), gamma(omega1)=sqrt(d), and
   gamma(omega2)=1/sqrt(d) follow from both shifts and reflection.
3. The sample reflection, generic integer shift, exceptional origin limit,
   and reciprocal W signs match the factorized finite coefficients.
4. The four gamma arguments in the reciprocal Fourier product have the
   stated complementary sums. The sine ratio sign is correct: its two
   arguments sum to an integer M, so the ratio is (-1)^(M+1), equal
   to the printed (-1)^((h-n)/2+k). The denominator argument lies in
   (0,1) for the reduced odd-h/principal-k range.
   A minor wording correction was sent to the author: both period shifts
   are used, not only omega2.
5. Transforming the convolution gives the phase that cancels the
   reciprocal Fourier product. The h=0 sum is
   n-2tau+tau^2 delta(g,0), and the dimension equation gives precisely
   tau^2[delta(g,0)-delta^(-1)].
6. At n=3 the displayed B matrix agrees entry by entry with the W ansatz.
   The h=1 quadratic sums are 2+pq,1-p-q,1-p-q. The selected roots
   p>0,q<0 have p+q=1,pq=delta, and tau^2=delta+2. The sample
   identity p=F(1)^3/sqrt(d) has the correct sign and normalization.
