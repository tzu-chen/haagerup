Checks passed by hand
--------------------
1. The generalized fusion ring, dimension equation and global dimension
   have the correct dependence on the group order. The comparison with
   near-group rules is useful and prevents a common confusion.

2. The determinant and endpoint signs cancel correctly on changing p,
   q or g lifts. For example, under q -> q+2z the first kernel summand
   has exponent det(p,z)+(q-p)^T Mz-q^T Mz = z^T Mp modulo two.
   The exceptional term acquires that same exponent, and the endpoint
   signs cancel it. The zero-class reciprocity is 1 rather than delta,
   precisely as required by the correction term.

3. The three axis values, axis sum and off-axis pair product are correct.
   Pairwise reciprocity is expressly separated from the summed quadratic
   and cubic equations, and the general lattice statement has the correct
   linear-only scope.

4. The Klein-four matrix agrees with the determinant ansatz, including
   the -i phase for p=(0,1), q=(1,0). Its first-column square sum reduces
   to 4/(delta+1)=5-delta=1-1/delta. Positive and negative embeddings are
   distinguished correctly. The odd-m inverse-pair count is 2m-2, and
   character normalization removes two of the three involution signs.

5. The order-eight eliminants imply the two positive square-root
   identities. With s=t^2 and s^2+8s-1=0,
       (9+10s+s^2)/(4s) = ((9+s)/2)^2,
       (13+s)/2 = ((5+s)/2)^2.
   The selected reciprocal root is the stated lambda_8^2. The displayed
   quartic and eliminant indeed imply tr^3=1-2r, so y^2 r^3=1.
   The exercise explicitly supplies the latter identity; its solution
   does not silently assume an unassigned elimination.

6. For order twelve, the cubic has the claimed unique root in (3,4).
   The bounds 2/7<t<3/10 suffice for endpoint signs and a positive
   derivative. Substituting u=C-C^{-1} produces the stated sextic:
   C^3 u^3=C^6-3C^4+3C^2-1 supplies the easily missed 3 terms.
   The monic iterated quotient is a 24-dimensional rational algebra
   regardless of irreducibility. Requiring denominator units rather than
   mere nonvanishing at one real embedding is the right condition for
   transporting the certificate to every positive embedding.

7. At t=i sqrt(delta_+), conjugation t -> -t, C -> C^{-1} and the
   norm identities imply the claimed Hermitian coefficient relation.
   The pointwise coefficient product is nonzero off the axes; the
   diagonal and zero row/column are real. The Q-system index is
   1+delta_+=7+sqrt(37), and six coefficient embeddings are not
   mislabeled as six inequivalent categories.

8. Multiplication by D-1 on (1,omega) has columns
   (2m-2,2) and (2m^2,2m). Its determinant is -4m and entry gcd is 2.
   Hence the Smith factors are 2 and 2m. The order-three example and
   all three exercise solutions are correct.

9. The newly added free-product mechanism agrees with the supplied
   reconstruction source: opposite translations on D_1,D_2; orthogonal
   Leavitt corners define the extension; covariance reindexes g+2h;
   the positive-degree, left-invertible intertwiner lemma removes new
   free-product letters. Algebraic faithfulness is correctly separated
   from positive analytic reconstruction.

One small pedagogical repair suggested
-------------------------------------
f06:294--295 and 328--329:
The text introduces z_j only as "normalized weights" and later infers
that every nonzero W has squared modulus delta_+. Readers cannot see
that last implication without the structural form of the weight table.
It would be enough to add:
  "In the prescribed representative table, the nonzero-residue entries
   are t, t z_j, and signed reciprocals t/z_j."
The full degree-five polynomial formula for the z_j need not be printed.
This makes the displayed norm implication self-contained while preserving
the intended finite-certificate input.
