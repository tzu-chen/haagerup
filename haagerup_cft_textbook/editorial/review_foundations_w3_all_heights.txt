1. The affine dimension gap (draft lines 58--85)
------------------------------------------------
Write x=pi/n and c=n-a-b, and sort a<=b<=c. Then
2a+b<=a+2b<=n, so both derivative sines are nonnegative. For a>=2,
first reducing a to 2 and then b to 2 stays in the sorted region;
for a=1, reducing b to 3 also stays there. Thus the claimed reductions
do not cross a region where the derivative argument would fail.

The quantum-integer identity [2]^2=[3]+1 verifies the strict ratio
D_n(2,2)/D_n(1,3)>1. Putting t=cos(2pi/n) gives
D_n(1,3)=2t+4t^2. For n>=12, t>=sqrt(3)/2, so the bound is
3+sqrt(3). The fundamental value 1+2t lies in [1+sqrt(3),3),
leaving d between the two dimension ranges. The lower bound on a
product of two noninvertible simples is also correct.

both denominator sine factors decrease as n grows, so this is the
minimum for all n>=12. No sampled-height argument is being used.
