Overall judgment
----------------
The new analytic definitions substantially improve the chapter. A reader
with the CFT and VOA primers can follow the core lemma, oscillator damping,
range/density argument, ordinary-module reconstruction, and the paired
rotation cancellation. The chapter now distinguishes three genuinely
different tasks: positive module norms, comparison of actual represented
operators, and positive fusion forms. It does not replace the latter two
with a simple-object count.

No new mathematical error found. The following small definition bridges

1. r04:71--85 and 212--216: make local vacuum cyclicity explicit
----------------------------------------------------------------
The net definition says only "The vacuum is cyclic." Later the separating
argument needs N_H(I') Omega to be dense for this particular nonempty
interval, not merely cyclicity for the global observable algebra.

Suggested insertion near the first definition:
  "For a nonempty interval the vacuum is also cyclic for its local algebra:
   N(I) Omega is dense (the Reeh--Schlieder property). This local statement,
   supplied by the conformal-net framework, is stronger than global
   vacuum cyclicity."
Then the separating proof is completely intelligible from definitions
already present. The proof itself is correct: y b Omega=b y Omega=0
on a dense set and boundedness give y=0.

2. r04:96--110 and 219--224: give statistical dimension one concrete meaning
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The terms "statistical dimension", "two-interval index", and later "index"
appear before a numerical interpretation. They are all standard, but a
beginning physics student may otherwise read them as three unrelated counts.
One short sentence after the sector paragraph would suffice:
  "For a localized sector represented by an endomorphism rho, its
   statistical dimension is the square root of the corresponding local
   subfactor index, d(rho)^2=[N(I):rho(N(I))]; it is a categorical size,
   not the number of states in an energy eigenspace."
If desired, refer back to the finite-index discussion in the foundations.
This is pedagogical context, not a request to reprove index theory.

3. r04:479--485: define Haag duality where the term first does work
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The chain of inclusions is excellent, but the condition is only named.
Add a short equation or phrase:
  "Haag duality says A(I')'=A(I) in the vacuum representation: every
   bounded operator commuting with the complementary local algebra
   already belongs to the interval algebra."
The existing displayed chain then becomes a complete, easily checked
argument. Strong locality supplies one inclusion; Haag duality supplies
the reverse completion.

4. r04:274--290: one backward reference is useful
------------------------------------------------
The ordinary/weak-module distinction is central here and is already
defined carefully in ch:vertex_algebras and ch:regularity. A parenthesis
"in the sense of Chapter ... " at the first reconstructed weak module
would help a reader who enters this chapter after the branching calculation.
No further redefinition is necessary.

Hand checks and pedagogical successes
------------------------------------
* The core definition uses the graph norm, and the proof tracks both
  components of graph convergence. Reducing T and 1-P really does give
  the adjoint statement; no hidden core hypothesis for T* is required.
* A bounded-energy cut-off space is used before ordinaryness is proved.
  The later projection argument justifies finite energy multiplicities,
  rather than building them into the net-sector assumption.
* The oscillator norm is the supremum of squared shift weights. It gives
  an immediate physical model for annulus damping without treating
  the inverse damping operator as bounded.
* The annulus range lemma is correct. Its equality of subspaces uses
  lambda^{-1} r^{-L0} M^fin=M^fin. The geometric subdisc paragraph
  now supplies the hypotheses instead of assuming them for arbitrary
  bounded operators.
* For fixed input p, output q and homogeneous field weight k,
  n=p+k-q-1 is correct. Finite sums of such states give a finite
  Laurent polynomial. The component argument uses an open nonempty
  overlap with the comparison regions and is suitable for the elementary
  identity theorem.
* The tensor-product boundedness requirement for localized insertions
  controls the inserted state as well as the state on which it acts.
  This distinction is made explicitly, and the analytic theorem remains
  a named input rather than an unproved consequence of polynomial
  energy bounds alone.
* The paired localizer formulas have the correct opposite deck phases.
  Since a VOA mode shifts energy by an integer, Z commutes with it;
  the pair insertion is invariant. The h=1/3 exercise is a useful
  minimal worked check of a phase invisible in the vacuum.
* The last section explains why complete unitarity permits the positive
  Q-system and the specified H3 extension. It correctly preserves the
  distinction between isomorphic ambient E8 theories and the particular
  embedding of U.

No expansion of the operator-algebra proof machinery is needed for this
audience beyond the three short definition bridges above.
