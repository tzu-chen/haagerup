% 1. TWO DIFFERENT BOXED TENSOR PRODUCTS -- highest priority.
% First category-product use: c03_factors.tex, immediately following
% Rep(P) ~= Fib boxtimes C(Z/3,-r^2/3), currently around lines 144--146.
% r02 has already defined boxtimes_V as fusion inside Rep(V), but neither
% this category product nor their distinction appears in r09.

Here $\boxtimes$ between two categories denotes their external
tensor product: its simple objects are pairs $X\boxtimes Y$,
and fusion and braiding act separately in the two factors.
In particular, dimensions and twists multiply. This differs from
$M\boxtimes_VN$, which fuses two modules inside the single category
$\Rep(V)$.

% Add one row in r09's Categories and modular data table; it is enough
% to summarize the distinction there without repeating the explanation.

$\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal D$, $M\boxtimes_VN$&
The first is the external product of two categories, with pairs of
simple labels. The second is the fusion product of two modules of
one VOA $V$. Neither means the plain tensor product of the two
underlying module vector spaces.\\

% 2. ENERGY GAP VERSUS SCALAR SCALING GAP -- high priority.
% l02_scaling.tex after eq:gap-dictionary, before the corrections paragraph.
% The book carefully distinguishes these operationally, but never gives
% a compact definition of "gapped" despite using it throughout Parts IV/V.

An energy gap and a conformal scaling gap are different quantities.
A gapped chain has a positive lower bound, independent of sufficiently
large $L$, on excitation energies above its vacuum sector. With several
pure vacua, that sector may be a cluster of nearly degenerate finite-volume
states; their small internal splittings do not define the bulk gap.
A CFT may have a positive lowest nonidentity scalar dimension
$\Delta_{\min}$ while remaining gapless, since its corresponding energy
difference is $2\pi v\Delta_{\min}/L$.

% Short r09 glossary entry, if desired instead of duplicating the paragraph:

\item[Gap.]An energy gap is an excitation-energy separation above the
vacuum sector. A scalar scaling gap is the lowest nonidentity scalar
dimension $\Delta$. A positive scaling gap in a CFT corresponds to
energies of order $1/L$, not a nonzero infinite-volume energy gap.

% 3. CONFORMAL BLOCK -- repair the existing r09 definition.
% Current entry defines every block as one intermediate-representation
% contribution. That is a useful description of a channel basis element,
% but it excludes the selected linear combinations explicitly called
% blocks in r06 and obscures multiplicity labels in r08.
% Replace the existing entry, rather than adding another one.

\item[Conformal block.]A chiral solution of the symmetry Ward identities
for specified external representations. A factorization basis labels
blocks by intermediate representations and junction multiplicities,
and sums their descendants. A general block, including one selected
by a lattice extension, may be a linear combination of that basis.
Full correlators additionally specify how holomorphic and
antiholomorphic blocks are paired.

% 4. WEAK INTEGRALITY -- first used in c06_cosets.tex around lines 271--277.
% No definition exists in the chapters or glossary. The construction
% agent is already handling the full-faithfulness cross-reference needed
% to apply the simple-object bound to a categorical action; do not replace
% that premise check by this definition.
% Suggested short first-use paragraph, or the glossary entry below.

A fusion category is \emph{integral} if all simple
Frobenius--Perron dimensions are integers, and \emph{weakly integral}
if its global Frobenius--Perron dimension is an integer.
In a weakly integral fusion category over $\C$, each simple object's
Frobenius--Perron dimension has integer square. For example, the Ising dimensions
$1,1,\sqrt2$ give global dimension four: this category is weakly
integral but not integral. The square condition concerns simple
objects; a reducible object's dimension is a sum of simple dimensions.

% r09 entry could be just:

\item[Weakly integral.]A fusion category whose global
Frobenius--Perron dimension is an integer. Its simple Frobenius--Perron dimensions
have integer squares, but need not themselves be integers;
the Ising dimension $\sqrt2$ is the basic example.

% 5. THE ROOT-OF-UNITY ARITHMETIC VOCABULARY -- c05_branching.tex.
% Place before the first conductor-forty orbit estimate (currently ~917).
% The reading guide assumes elementary algebra, not Galois theory.
% f05 explains field automorphisms much later; the following is enough
% to read the immediate orbit-averaging mechanism without a new chapter.

Here is the arithmetic vocabulary used in this last step.
A \emph{cyclotomic field} $\Q(\zeta_N)$ is generated by
$\zeta_N=e^{2\pi i/N}$. Its automorphisms send
$\zeta_N$ to $\zeta_N^a$ with $\gcd(a,N)=1$, preserving every
rational polynomial relation. If a subfield $K$ is fixed, averaging
over the automorphisms that fix $K$ lands in $K$.
For example, averaging the two conjugates of $\zeta_3$ gives
$(\zeta_3+\zeta_3^2)/2=-1/2$.
The \emph{conductor} of the abelian number field considered here
is the smallest $N$ for which it lies in $\Q(\zeta_N)$.

A root of order exactly $N$ is called primitive. Summing all
primitive $N$th roots gives the Ramanujan sum $\mu(N)$ used below.
A quadratic Gauss sum instead weights roots by a quadratic
character: modulo an odd prime $p$, this character is $1$ on
nonzero squares, $-1$ on nonsquares, and $0$ at zero. Such weighted
sums isolate the square-root part of the orbit average.

% 6. COMPLETELY POSITIVE -- l04_tensor_networks.tex around lines 238--244.
% The first transfer-map formula is explicit, but the adjective has no
% definition. Insert immediately after that formula.

\emph{Completely positive} means that
$\mathrm{id}_{M_r(\C)}\otimes\mathcal E$ preserves positive
semidefinite matrices for every auxiliary dimension $r$.
The displayed formula has this property because each summand
acts as $Z\mapsto(1\otimes A^s)Z(1\otimes A^s)^\dagger$.
A transfer map need not preserve the ordinary matrix trace.

% r09 glossary entry, if wanted:

\item[Completely positive map.]A linear map that preserves
positive semidefinite matrices even after tensoring with the identity
map on any finite auxiliary matrix space. The MPS transfer formula
$\mathcal E(X)=\sum_sA^sX(A^s)^\dagger$ makes this property explicit;
trace preservation is a separate condition.

% 7. PERRON EIGENVECTOR -- after the Fibonacci eigenvalue example in f01,
% or as a glossary-only entry. The many later "Perron" weights and
% probabilities currently rely on terminology not explicitly explained.
% This is the ordinary nonnegative-matrix theorem, not a substitute
% for the separate completely-positive-map hypotheses in l04.

This is an example of the Perron--Frobenius theorem.
For a nonnegative matrix whose directed graph is strongly connected,
the spectral radius $r=\max_\lambda|\lambda|$ is a simple eigenvalue
with strictly positive left and right eigenvectors, unique up to scale.
These are its \emph{Perron eigenvectors}. If some power of the matrix
has every entry strictly positive, the matrix is primitive and every
other eigenvalue has modulus less than $r$. The Fibonacci fusion
matrix above has this property.

% A short r09 entry can replace the chapter insertion if length is tight.

\item[Perron eigenvector.]A positive eigenvector of a nonnegative
matrix at its spectral radius. For a matrix with strongly connected
directed graph it is unique up to scale; primitivity additionally
excludes other eigenvalues of the same modulus. Such vectors supply
the positive weights in fusion graphs and classical transfer matrices.
