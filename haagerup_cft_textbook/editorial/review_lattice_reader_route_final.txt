ASSESSMENT

The guide accurately describes the actual order. Part I gives fusion
spaces, CFT, lattice VOAs, the six Haagerup lines, centers, and modular
data. Part II develops the explicit construction through unitarity and
then characters. Part III treats full theories and sewing. Part IV
starts with cosets and then treats the physical chain. The existing
Ising tour is particularly effective: it supplies one completely
specified comparison theory across otherwise different mathematical
languages, without implying that it is the Haagerup chain.

No global chapter reordering is needed. The printed order is sensible
for a reader following the existence argument. It nevertheless delays
the first concrete physical-chain calculation until Chapter 19 and
places the long coset frontier before it. A beginning physics reader
can profitably visit the exact Ising calibration and the elementary
fusion-path construction earlier, then return to the research
construction. The current prose route at r00:170-178 allows this but
does not give actual chapter references or a checkable result to work
toward.

I drafted one optional eight-row route table in
editorial/physics_reading_route_table.tex.txt. It names existing
chapter labels, gives a concrete skill at each stage, and returns to
the existence proofs rather than silently bypassing their hypotheses.
The row on structural proofs deliberately describes following the
logical dependencies, rather than promising that one short
calculation proves rationality or complete unitarity.

The table is intended immediately after the existing Ising-tour
paragraph, or as a replacement for the physics half of the general
route paragraph. It supplements the printed order, not the chapter
contents. It should not be accompanied by a proposed number of days
or hours to master the material.

FOUR TARGETED PREREQUISITE BRIDGES

1. Bogoliubov normal form and the fermionic zero-point energy.
   Most useful actual addition.
   Location: l02_scaling.tex:148-188.

   The particle-hole matrix, parity preservation, and NS/R projection
   are explained well. The transition from its positive eigenvalue to
   E_0=-(1/2) sum_k epsilon(k) assumes familiarity with the normal form
   of a quadratic fermion Hamiltonian. A beginning reader may otherwise
   wonder why the sum counts all allowed k yet contains a factor 1/2.
   A two-sentence bridge after the Bogoliubov explanation would close
   the gap:

   "The transformation is canonical: the new operators gamma_k obey
   the same fermion anticommutation relations. The diagonal Hamiltonian
   is H_I=sum_k epsilon(k)(gamma_k^dagger gamma_k-1/2), with each
   allowed quasiparticle momentum counted once; its empty-mode energy
   is therefore minus one half of the dispersion sum."

   Retain the following R-sector paragraph: its required zero-mode
   occupancy is precisely the parity-projection exception to the
   empty-mode description, and costs zero energy at criticality.
   This is a missing intermediate step, not a correction to the
   energies already proved.

2. Affine level, GKO subtraction, and the diagonal level forty.
   A cross-reference is enough; do not rederive the whole theory.
   Location: c06_cosets.tex:323-341.

   The reader meets "compact diagonal" and "GKO stress" after many
   intervening chapters. The relevant prerequisites already appear in
   r02_vertex_algebras.tex:560-621 and the Ising branching example
   ex:voa-Ising-branching; c03_factors.tex:45-64 explains the finite
   integrable label set and distinguishes affine fusion from top-spin
   tensor products.

   Suggested short reminder:
   "Here GKO means the diagonal affine coset construction used in
   Worked example [ex:voa-Ising-branching]: the current double poles
   add the levels, while the coset stress subtracts the diagonal
   Sugawara stress from the two numerator stresses."

   This makes the level 1+39=40 and the displayed central-charge
   subtraction immediately reconstructible. The existing rational
   identification and Haagerup-preservation caveats remain necessary.

3. PCT-real versus coordinatewise real.
   Optional backward cross-reference.
   Location: c06_cosets.tex:20-22 and :331.

   PCT is not undefined in the book: r02_vertex_algebras.tex:701-726
   explains its radial-adjoint meaning and transport under a gauge
   change; c01_lattice_embedding.tex treats the actual compact gauge.
   But a physics reader jumping to reduced theories may confuse
   PCT-real with real coefficients in the displayed exponential basis.

   A parenthetical at first use is sufficient:
   "PCT-real means fixed by the antilinear radial-adjoint involution
   of Chapter [ch:vertex_algebras], with the positive form transported
   in the chosen gauge."

   No separate spacetime-PCT digression is needed. The chapter already
   gives a good definition of semiconformality and of the commutant.

4. Direct integrals and partition densities.
   Useful one-sentence definition before the strict continuum result.
   Location: c06_cosets.tex:672-690.

   The text correctly distinguishes a partition density from a literal
   finite Hilbert-space trace, and states the positive-measure and
   modular-inversion hypotheses. The term "direct integral" itself is
   introduced without a beginner's definition.

   Suggested bridge:
   "A direct integral is the continuum analogue of a direct sum of
   sectors: a measure replaces the sum over module labels. Integrating
   their characters with that measure defines a partition density,
   whose normalization and modular law require the assumptions stated
   below."

   Avoid asserting that a finite Laplace integral is automatically the
   trace of the continuous Hilbert-space operator, or that every
   noncompact theory has a unique canonical density. The present
   conditional proposition deliberately does not assume either claim.

ALREADY ADEQUATELY EXPLAINED

No new prerequisite sections are needed for ordinary versus categorical
symmetry, constrained path Hilbert spaces, NS versus R boundary
conditions, conformal spin versus lattice momentum, Schmidt
decomposition, current-current marginal running, Sugawara's basic
formula, or branching grade. Their local explanations and existing
references now give sufficient entry points. The compact-versus-
noncompact and all-branches-diagonal hypotheses in the coset chapter
are also stated carefully. The remaining challenge is the amount of
material, which a concrete route can address better than further
terminology or warnings.
