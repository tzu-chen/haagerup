## Apply the actual mirror theorem

Since R and C are strongly rational and their stresses sum to that
of B, B is a finite semisimple R tensor C module. Write its canonical
isotypical decomposition as

\[
 B=\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i\otimes N_i,
\]

where the M_i are distinct R simples and the N_i are initially
allowed to be semisimple C modules. Mutual commutants give exactly
the unit-multiplicity hypotheses of
[McRae, Theorem1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.06577).
All module-category and tensor-product assumptions are available
because both VOAs are strongly rational. That theorem proves that
the R simples occurring in B form a fusion subcategory, and that
the N_i are distinct simple objects forming a braid-reversed
equivalent fusion subcategory of Rep(C).

Because y3 occurs, its fusion square shows I consists of all fourteen
R simples. Thus the resulting subcategory D of Rep(C) is braided
equivalent to Rep(R) reversed. It is nondegenerate. The equivalence
also reverses the conformal twists: B has integer grading, so
theta_(M_i) theta_(N_i)=1. This is explicitly noted after the proof
of Theorem4.6 in the same source.

## Split off the nine ambient sectors

Let P be the Müger centralizer of D inside Rep(C). The modular
factorization theorem gives

\[
 \operatorname{Rep}(C)\simeq D\boxtimes P.
\]

In Rep(R) tensor D tensor P the extension algebra B is supported
entirely in the first two factors. Its underlying object is the
diagonal sum over all fourteen R simples. Its FP dimension is
Dim Rep(R). Its categorical dimension is the same positive number:
the mirror reverses twists as well as braiding and preserves the
positive dimensions of the known R category, so the sum is
sum_i dim(M_i) squared. Thus the extension has nonzero categorical
dimension, and the standard separability/local-module theorem
applies; there is no omitted zero-index exception. In
Rep(R) tensor D the algebra is Lagrangian. The category
of its local modules in those first two factors is therefore Vec.
The local-module category of B in the complete product is P.

The VOA extension correspondence identifies that local-module
category with Rep(B). Hence P is braided equivalent to Rep(B),
proving the boxed factorization. This argument uses an existing
extension algebra and its local modules; it does not assume an
orbifold anomaly vanishes or posit a splitting of a group action
on an abstract modular category.

For references on this final categorical step, see
[Creutzig--Kanade--McRae, Gluing vertex algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00119)
for the diagonal algebra formulation and
[Müger, On the structure of modular categories](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0201017)
for modular centralizer factorization. The standard local-module
correspondence is the one already used throughout the extension
and core notes.

## What this does and does not settle for U

The fourteen branching spaces N_i are now actual simple C modules,
not merely candidate characters. The B extension over R tensor C
has the complete diagonal form. If U contains all of C, then its
right currents give its y3 summand under R tensor C. R and C remain
mutual commutants in U, so the same mirror theorem makes the R labels
occurring in U fusion closed. They must include all fourteen. Every
corresponding R tensor C summand occurs only once in B, so U=B. Thus a proper
U must have a proper residual subalgebra Com(R,U) inside C.

The converse is immediate. This reduces properness to a concrete
subalgebra problem inside the now-identified rational c=18/5 coset.
It does not assert that Com(R,U) is rational, nor that knowing its
two commuting Virasoro stresses q and r determines all its fields.
